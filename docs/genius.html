Genius and major prizes

Does a prize winner meet the Euler / Feynman / Nash / Einstein bar?

A first-pass retrospective classification produced by ChatGPT on 11 October 2026.

At this high threshold, the conservative tally is about 86% for Abel, 59% for Fields, 47% for Turing, and 2–17% across the Nobel categories. The arithmetic counts an explicitly stated set of judgments about the laureates. The judgments require much more interpretation than the arithmetic.

Prize records are factual; genius classifications are judgments. This is one assistant’s screen of complete laureate lists, informed by selected biographies. It has no independent raters, measured IQ or exhaustive biographical review. The percentages are frequencies under that coding.

The threshold

The benchmark is exceptional individual conceptual or creative originality, with enduring stature comparable to Feynman or Nash. Euler and Einstein mark the exceptional upper end. Winning an important award is insufficient by itself: the assessment asks whether the person’s originality warrants the description independently of the prize. Euler is a calibration example; he lived before these awards.

A clear case is a comfortable affirmative judgment at that threshold. A borderline case has a plausible claim under a more generous reading. Not established here means the person is outside the affirmative shortlist; it does not establish absence of genius. Organizations are marked not applicable.

For Literature, the assessment includes comparable artistic originality. That comparison is especially subjective and makes no claim about the laureate’s scientific or mathematical ability.

The comparison

The population is all distinct people within each prize, through award year 2025. Fields therefore ends at its 2022 awards. Repeat awards within a category count once, shared prizes contribute each individual, and Peace organizations are excluded from the denominator. Perelman is included as a Fields honoree although he declined the medal. Abel’s honorary pre-2003 award is outside the population.

The strict frequency is clear cases / distinct people. The inclusive frequency is (clear + borderline cases) / distinct people. The second is a sensitivity endpoint, not a confidence interval or a rigorous upper bound on the prevalence of genius.

The names behind the counts

Some choices make the threshold easier to assess. In Economics, the eight clear cases are Nash, Arrow, Samuelson, Kantorovich, Simon, Debreu, Aumann and Shapley. Friedman, Hayek, Coase, Markowitz and Kahneman are among the borderline cases. In Abel, Varadhan, Meyer, Uhlenbeck and Caffarelli are the four borderline cases. Calling all four geniuses is entirely defensible; the strict tally withholds “unequivocal” at the stated threshold.

Peace’s two clear cases, Pauling and Sakharov, qualify through their scientific originality. Literature’s clear cases include Faulkner, Beckett and García Márquez, assessed as creative minds. These are coding decisions, not endorsements supplied by the prize organizations.

The complete audit

The audit has 1,193 rows: 1,165 person-category records and 28 distinct Peace organizations. Each row records the prize, name, award years, recipient type, classification and rationale. The organization rows preserve the full roster while contributing nothing to the person counts.

Editable spreadsheet Complete audit CSV

In the spreadsheet, change Coding on the Laureates tab to challenge an individual judgment. The counts and shares on the Comparison tab recalculate. The CSV provides the same row-level classifications for independent calculations.

Reproduce the calculation

Download the CSV and run this Python code in the same directory. It uses the standard library and calculates both columns from the recorded labels.

from collections import Counter
import csv

people = Counter()
clear = Counter()
borderline = Counter()
with open("genius-prize-audit.csv", encoding="utf-8", newline="") as f:
    for row in csv.DictReader(f):
        if row["Recipient type"] != "Person":
            continue
        prize = row["Prize"]
        people[prize] += 1
        clear[prize] += row["Coding"] == "Clear"
        borderline[prize] += row["Coding"] == "Borderline"

for prize, n in people.items():
    strict = clear[prize] / n
    inclusive = (clear[prize] + borderline[prize]) / n
    print(f"{prize}: {clear[prize]}/{n}, {strict:.1%}, {inclusive:.1%}")

What the numbers can support

Classification uncertainty dominates. Familiarity with a field, the visibility of a person’s work, historical reputation and the meaning of intellectual versus artistic originality can all change the labels. Screening an entire roster does not remove those biases. Independent raters working to a more detailed common rubric would provide a stronger assessment.

All remaining Abel and Fields laureates were marked borderline. Their 100% inclusive values are therefore permissive endpoints of this coding, not evidence that every recipient unequivocally meets the benchmark. The Nobel and Turing borderline lists are also judgments, not exhaustive sets of all possible claims.

The prize populations differ in era, career stage, age restrictions and selection criteria. These are retrospective judgments about lifetime work, including work after a prize was awarded, rather than forecasts about the latest winner. The broad separation in this particular coding is more credible than the fine ordering among Nobel categories.

The direction of conditioning also matters. A frequency among prize winners does not give the probability that a genius wins a prize, and gives no estimate of genius prevalence in the general population.

This question sits beside winning’s concern with the relationship between observed winners and latent qualities. Here, the classifications were assigned by judgment and counted directly. No race or ability model has been fitted to prize decisions.

Nobel rosters were parsed from Wikipedia and reconciled with Nobel Foundation totals. Physics has 230 award-recipient entries through 2025 but 229 people because Bardeen appears twice. Chemistry has 200 entries but 198 people because Sanger and Sharpless each appear twice. Peace has 112 people and 31 organization awards to 28 distinct organizations.
