Order-statistic queries and evidence, such as which of several correlated variables is largest or who won a contest, have long resisted exact treatment in Bayes nets. For correlated Gaussian variables the probability that one is the maximum is an orthant probability, which Bayesian optimization calls intractable and approximates; rating systems such as TrueSkill approximate their posteriors by expectation propagation. When the dependence among Gaussian variables runs through a few shared factors, a recent algorithm for correlated contests computes the probability that each variable is the largest deterministically, at a cost linear in the number of variables at fixed factor rank and quadrature resolution, together with its derivatives. We cast that computation as a win node in a Bayes net and add the evidence such a node meets in practice: an observed winner, a quoted price and a published place, with their likelihoods and posterior updates. We also show that the posteriors of simulation under common random numbers and of a bandit whose arms share features stay in factor form after every update. Under a posterior, the probabilities are the action probabilities of Thompson sampling.
