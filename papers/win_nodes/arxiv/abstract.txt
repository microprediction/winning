Order-statistic queries and evidence, such as which of several correlated variables is largest or who won a contest, have long resisted exact treatment in Bayes nets. For correlated Gaussian variables the probability that one is the maximum is an orthant probability, which Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning call intractable and approximate, and which rating systems reach only through expectation propagation. We show that when the dependence among Gaussian variables runs through a few shared factors, or along a Markov chain, the probability that each variable is the largest can be computed deterministically at a cost linear in the number of variables. In the factor case, which rests on a recent algorithm for correlated contests, the same computation gives the derivatives of these probabilities and the likelihood of an observed winner. Under a posterior, the probabilities are the action probabilities of Thompson sampling.
