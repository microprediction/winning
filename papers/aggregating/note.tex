\begin{abstract}
\noindent Vovk's Aggregating Algorithm predicts with a substitution of
its weighted mixture of experts. For the log-loss of a contest winner
under a Thurstone race model, the mixture of races over Gaussian
uncertainty in the abilities is again a race, with that uncertainty
added to the performance covariance. The algorithm's prediction under a
Gaussian posterior is therefore one forward pass, linear in the field
size when the covariance has factor structure, where the softmax
analogue needs Monte Carlo. We state the fact and check it
numerically. Online, the mixed prediction was no more accurate than
the plug-in race at the posterior mean, even with the exact posterior;
what it supplies is the algorithm's worst-case guarantee at the cost
of one race.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

A contest has $n$ entrants with abilities $x\in\mathbb R^n$. Entrant
$i$ performs $Y_i=x_i+\varepsilon_i$ with $\varepsilon\sim
N(0,\Sigma)$, and the lowest performance wins. Write $p_\Sigma(x)$ for
the vector of win probabilities and
\[
  \ell(x,y)=-\ln p_{\Sigma,y}(x)
\]
for the log-loss of an observed winner $y$. When $\Sigma=V V^\top+
\operatorname{diag}(D)$ with $V$ of low rank, $p_\Sigma(x)$ costs time
linear in $n$, and so does its inverse $p_\Sigma^{-1}$, which returns
the abilities (up to translation) that give a target vector of win
probabilities \citep{cotton2026}. The race map is onto the open
simplex, so the inverse is defined everywhere.

An online learner sees contests $t=1,\dots,T$, each a field
$F_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}$ from a population of $N$ entrants, and
predicts a distribution over the winner before $y_t$ is revealed.

\section{The Aggregating Algorithm and its substitution}

The Aggregating Algorithm \citep{vovk1990,vovk2001} keeps weights $P_t$
over experts, here ability vectors $x$, updated as
$P_{t+1}(x)\propto P_t(x)\,e^{-\eta\,\ell(x_{F_t},y_t)}$, and predicts
with a substitution $s(P_t)$. The loss is $\eta$-mixable if some $s$
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mix}
  \ell(s(P),y)\le-\tfrac1\eta\ln\mathbb E_{x\sim P}\,
  e^{-\eta\,\ell(x,y)}\qquad\text{for all }P,\,y,
\end{equation}
and then the cumulative loss is at most
$-\tfrac1\eta\ln\mathbb E_{x\sim P_1}e^{-\eta L_T(x)}$, where $L_T(x)$ is
expert $x$'s cumulative loss. Against any comparator distribution $Q$
this is at most $\mathbb E_Q L_T+\tfrac1\eta\operatorname{KL}(Q\|P_1)$.

At $\eta=1$ the right side of \eqref{eq:mix} is $-\ln\mathbb E_P\,
p_{\Sigma,y}(x)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sub}
  s(P)=p_\Sigma^{-1}\!\big(\mathbb E_{x\sim P}\,p_\Sigma(x)\big)
\end{equation}
meets \eqref{eq:mix} with equality for every $y$. This is not new.
Log-loss is 1-mixable whatever the parameterisation of the forecast,
provided the parameterisation reaches every interior forecast
\citep[Section~6]{vanerven2012}, and \citet{foster2018} give the
softmax case, $s(\pi)=\sigma^{-1}(\mathbb E_\pi\sigma(x))$, as the
route to logarithmic regret for multiclass logistic regression. The
Thurstone case needs only that $p_\Sigma$ is invertible, and
\eqref{eq:sub} is computable with the inverse above.

\section{Gaussian closure}

The step that is specific to races is the expectation in
\eqref{eq:sub}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:closure}
If $x\sim N(m,S)$ then $\mathbb E\,p_\Sigma(x)=p_{\Sigma+S}(m)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$Y=x+\varepsilon$ with independent $x\sim N(m,S)$ and
$\varepsilon\sim N(0,\Sigma)$ is $N(m,\Sigma+S)$, and averaging the
event $\{Y_y<Y_j\ \forall j\ne y\}$ over $x$ gives its probability
under that law.
\end{proof}

This is the multinomial-probit version of the familiar
$\mathbb E\,\Phi(w^\top z)=\Phi(m^\top z/\sqrt{1+z^\top Sz})$
\citep[Section~3.9]{rasmussen2006} and of random-coefficient probit
\citep{hausman1978,train2009}. Its consequence for aggregation is that
under a Gaussian posterior the algorithm's prediction is a single race,
with performance covariance $\Sigma+S$. If $S$ is itself low rank plus
diagonal, as a factor posterior is, $\Sigma+S$ keeps that form and the
prediction costs one forward pass, linear in $n$. Passing it through
$p_\Sigma^{-1}$ expresses it, if wanted, as abilities in the original
model.

The softmax has no such closure. The fast mixable method of
\citet{jezequel2021} runs the Aggregating Algorithm on quadratic
surrogates, so its posterior is exactly Gaussian, and then estimates
$\mathbb E\,\sigma(x)$ by sampling; they used 100 draws. For races the
same step is exact.

The closure also covers whole orderings. A Gaussian mixture of
Thurstone ranking laws with covariance $\Sigma$ is the Thurstone
ranking law with covariance $\Sigma+S$, so the ranking log-loss is
1-mixable with a substitution inside the general-covariance Thurstone
family. Plackett--Luce is not closed in this way: mixtures of
Plackett--Luce laws violate independence of irrelevant alternatives
\citep{train2009} and form a strictly larger class \citep{zhao2016}.
Since a substitution meeting \eqref{eq:mix} at $\eta=1$ for every
ordering must equal the mixture, the ranking log-loss with a
Plackett--Luce substitution is not 1-mixable. The closure needs the
general covariance: a Case~V model (independent, equal-variance noise)
is not closed, because $S$ adds correlation.

\section{What the guarantee covers}

The exact aggregating posterior under probit likelihoods is not
Gaussian; it is unified skew-normal \citep{durante2019}. The
one-pass prediction therefore applies exactly to a Gaussian posterior:
the surrogate posterior of \citet{jezequel2021}, or a Laplace or
expectation-propagation approximation, which carries no regret
guarantee of its own. For the exact Bayesian mixture with a Gaussian
prior, regret bounds that need only bounded curvature of the
log-likelihood \citep{kakade2005,kakade2005gp} apply to probit. Whether
improperness is needed at all for probit is less clear than for the
logistic, whose proper lower bound comes from its exponential tail.
We claim the computation, not a new rate, and the checks below find
no accuracy gain from it in ordinary rating streams.

\section{Checks}

All numbers come from \texttt{checks.py} and \texttt{exact\_bayes.py} with fixed seeds.

\emph{Substitution.} For random mixtures $P$ of 2 to 50 ability
vectors in fields of 3 to 25, deliberately non-Gaussian,
\eqref{eq:sub} computed with the race inverse meets \eqref{eq:mix} with
equality to within $8.5\times10^{-9}$ in every coordinate, the inverse's own
tolerance.

\emph{Closure.} For posteriors $N(m,LL^\top+\operatorname{diag}d)$ in
fields of 4 to 12 with $L$ of rank 1 or 2, the single race at
covariance $I+LL^\top+\operatorname{diag}d$ agrees with
$4\times10^6$ simulated contests to within $1.7$ standard errors in
every entry, at 1 to 20 ms per call.

\emph{Scale.} For factor posteriors over $1{,}000$ entrants (rank 2)
the mixed race takes $0.70$ s. Averaging 100 plug-in races over
posterior draws, the Monte Carlo step of \citet{jezequel2021}, takes
$0.74$ s and misses the ten favourites' probabilities by up to $25\%$
(median over five repetitions); reaching $1\%$ would need some $6\times
10^4$ draws. At $5{,}000$ entrants and rank 3 the mixed race takes
$53$ s, against $3.6$ s for 100 draws that miss by up to $53\%$.

\emph{Online.} Entrants with standard normal abilities race in fields
of 5 drawn from a population of $N$, for $T=1000$ contests, five
seeds. A Gaussian posterior is updated by one Laplace step per contest
on the field's block, and predicts either by the mixed race
$p_{I+S_{FF}}(m_F)$ or by the plug-in race $p_I(m_F)$. Summed over
the 1000 contests, the mixed race lost more than the plug-in by
$1.0\pm0.6$ nats at $N=20$ and $3.9\pm2.0$ nats at $N=400$, where each
entrant has about a dozen contests. A diagonal posterior did no
better. To separate the substitution from the Laplace approximation,
a small case ($N=6$, fields of 3, 40 contests, 30 seeds) was run with
the exact posterior on $6\times10^4$ prior particles. Exact Bayes, the
Aggregating Algorithm itself, and the plug-in race at the exact
posterior mean differed by $0.007\pm0.118$ nats, which is nothing; the Laplace
mixed race lost $0.23\pm0.09$ nats to it, the Laplace plug-in
$0.02\pm0.03$. In these tests the mixture's smoothing is second order,
and a crude Gaussian posterior overstates the variance it smooths
with.

\section{Remarks}

Glicko's expected score shrinks the rating difference by
$g(\sqrt{RD_i^2+RD_j^2})$ \citep{glickman1999}, and TrueSkill adds the
players' variances to the performance variance \citep{herbrich2006}.
Both are approximate Gaussian aggregating predictions for two players.
Proposition~\ref{prop:closure} gives the exact version for any number
of entrants with correlated performances and correlated uncertainty,
at the cost of one race.
