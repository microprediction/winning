Pass@$k$ is often extrapolated from a few scored attempts per prompt by the plug-in $1-(1-\hat p)^k$, which assigns zero to every prompt that has not yet succeeded. Chen et al. (2021) noted the bias, Kazdan et al. (2025) corrected the aggregate with a fitted difficulty prior, and V\'erine et al. (2026) published the per-prompt posterior predictive. We evaluate that estimator on nine thousand prompts of released rollouts, with two training attempts per prompt and six held out. The plug-in understates aggregate pass@6 by seventeen points, and a third of the $5{,}511$ prompts it rates unreachable succeed in the held-out attempts. Shrinking the point estimate accounts for most of the log-loss improvement, integrating over the posterior adds a smaller further gain, and a fixed Jeffreys prior obtains nearly all the gain of the fitted priors.
