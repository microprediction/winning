# Egan (1957) — monitoring task, confusion matrices for several message sets

## Citation

Egan, J. P. (1957). Monitoring Task in Speech Communication. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of
America*, **29**(4), 482-489. doi:10.1121/1.1908936
Hearing and Communication(s) Laboratory, Indiana University (same laboratory as Clarke 1957 and
Clarke & Anderson 1957; Egan was its director). Published April 1957 — two months *before* Clarke's
CRR paper in the same volume.

**This paper is not in the spine as usually drawn, and it should be.** It is one of only two datasets
that Morgan (1974) subjected to a proper likelihood-ratio test of Luce's choice axiom, and it failed.

## Stimuli and master response set

Speech messages in noise, in a **monitoring** paradigm: several noisy channels, only some carrying
relevant information; the listener must identify a restricted number of messages and ignore others.
Verbatim from the abstract:

> "The operator must receive and identify a restricted number of different messages, and he must
> ignore others."

Two experiments are reported. Verbatim: "*The results of two experiments are reported. In one of
these, the confusion matrices for the various sets of messages were also determined.*"

So **one of the two experiments yields confusion matrices for several message sets of differing
size**; the vocabulary and set sizes are not recoverable from the abstract.

## Restricted response sets (state whether nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested, by construction — set size is the independent variable.** The design manipulates the
"restricted number of different messages" the monitor must identify, and matrices were obtained "for
the various sets of messages". That is a master-plus-subsets family over a common speech vocabulary.

Additional structure specific to this paper, and unique in the branch: the monitor also has an
**accept/reject criterion** ("*the listener's criterion for accepting his response as correct or
rejecting it as incorrect*"), described via an operating characteristic and an
"articulation-criterion function". That means the response set is not purely a forced-choice
restriction — there is a rejection option. Whether the printed matrices are closed forced-choice
matrices or include a reject column is the key thing to check on retrieval, because a reject column
changes what "removing an alternative" means.

## What numbers are printed (master matrix? restricted matrices? counts or proportions? which table numbers?)

NOT DIRECTLY VERIFIED (paywalled, 8 pages). Established indirectly:

- **Confusion matrices for several message sets are printed** — the author's abstract says the
  matrices "were also determined", and Morgan (1974) could only run a likelihood-ratio test on
  transition matrices if the cell frequencies were in print. Townsend & Landon (1982, *JMP* 25, p.
  122): "*Morgan (1974) applied his likelihood ratio test to the data reported by Clarke (1957) and
  Egan (1957) and found their data to depart significantly from the predictions of the CRR.*"
- **Data pooled over subjects.** Townsend & Landon (1982, p. 122): "*Both Clarke (1957) and Egan
  (1957) presented their data pooled over subjects and reported that the CRR held with their data.*"
- Operating characteristics and an articulation-criterion function are also printed (as curves).
- Table numbers unknown. Eight pages is comfortable room for a master matrix plus several subset
  matrices, so the prospects here are better than for the 3-page Clarke & Anderson note.

A fuller version of the same work almost certainly exists as a technical report with more complete
matrices — see `egan1957_techreport5750.md` in this directory.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**NEEDS LIBRARY ACCESS, then digitizing. Promote to joint-top priority with Clarke (1957).**
Rationale: it is the *other* dataset Morgan (1974) showed to violate the axiom, it is 8 pages (so
likely to print full matrices), and it is the only member of the branch that varies set size inside a
monitoring/rejection paradigm. It has been effectively invisible in CRR reviews because its title
does not mention the constant-ratio rule.

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

Egan's abstract does not mention the constant-ratio rule at all (it predates the naming of the rule
by two months). Verbatim, in full:

> "Some communication situations involve several noisy channels, and only certain ones of these carry
> relevant information to a given communication operator. The operator must receive and identify a
> restricted number of different messages, and he must ignore others. The performance of the listener
> in this situation will depend both on the discriminability of the messages and on the listener's
> criterion for accepting his response as correct or rejecting it as incorrect. The present paper
> gives a quantitative description of the monitor's behavior in terms of the operating characteristic
> and the articulation-criterion function. The results of two experiments are reported. In one of
> these, the confusion matrices for the various sets of messages were also determined."

What later authors say Egan concluded, verbatim from Townsend & Landon (1982, *JMP* 25, p. 122):

> "Several tests of the CRR have been conducted, and most have concluded that the CRR does make
> satisfactory predictions with choice data (Anderson, Note 3; Clarke, 1957, 1959; Clarke & Anderson,
> 1957; Egan, 1957, Note 4; Hodge, 1967; Hodge & Pollack, 1962; Pollack & Decker, 1960)."

> "Both Clarke (1957) and Egan (1957) presented their data pooled over subjects and reported that the
> CRR held with their data. Morgan (1974) suggested that the discrepancy between his conclusion and
> the conclusions of Clarke and Egan might have been due to the use of pooled data, and could
> possibly be cleared up through the use of individual subject data and analysis."
