# Hodge & Pollack (1962) — auditory tone displays, 8x8 masters with 4x4 and 2x2 subsets, 9 conditions

## Citation

Hodge, M. H., & Pollack, I. (1962). Confusion matrix analysis of single and multidimensional auditory
displays. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, **63**(2), 129-142. doi:10.1037/h0042219
PMID 13907948.

The largest CRR experiment in the auditory branch by number of conditions, and the one that first
established the single- vs multi-dimensional split that Lee (1968) then tried to explain
theoretically. Cited in Townsend & Landon's (1982) canonical list of CRR tests.

## Stimuli and master response set

**Auditory tone displays, not speech.** Two families:

*Single-dimensional*: tones varying along one dimension only.
- **Duration** (one condition)
- **Intensity** (one condition)
- **Intensity with four spacing/range settings**, labelled series A, B, C, D — this is the paper's
  Experiment VII, which varies interstimulus spacing and ensemble range while holding set size fixed.

*Multidimensional*: tones varying on more than one dimension at once.
- **Coarse spacing**
- **Fine spacing**
- **Redundant objects** (dimensions correlated so as to be redundant)

**Master response set is 8 items in every condition (8x8 master matrices).** Established from Hodge
(1967), which reprints the Hodge & Pollack summary statistics in its Table 3 under the headings
"4 by 4" and "2 by 2" against an 8x8 master, footnoted "*a From Hodge and Pollack (1962)*", and
describes the design in the text (p. 431): "*A similar result was obtained with auditory stimuli by
Hodge and Pollack (1962).*"

## Restricted response sets (state whether nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested, at two depths: 8 -> 4 and 8 -> 2.** Every condition has an 8x8 master plus a family of 4x4
subset matrices and a family of 2x2 subset matrices, with stimulus set and allowable response set
restricted together.

The subsets are **overlapping families, not partitions** — Hodge's parallel visual experiments (which
followed the same protocol) used "*two sequences each of 7 ensembles of 4 objects, and one sequence
each of 14 ensembles of 2 objects*" per 8-item master, i.e. 7 different quadruples and 14 different
pairs drawn from the same 8. Note 14 pairs is a proper subfamily of the 28 available pairs, and the
pairs deliberately span a range of interstimulus spacings so that adjacent (highly confusable) and
distant (easily discriminable) pairs are both represented. Whether Hodge & Pollack used exactly 7 and
14 is not verified, but the reported "% diffs > .10" percentages in Hodge (1967) Table 3 take values
like 4.2, 2.8, 6.2, 9.4, 1.6, 12.5 for the 4x4 conditions and 28.6, 21.4, 10.0, 6.2, 5.0, 50.0 for the
2x2 conditions, whose denominators are consistent with families of a few dozen cells.

**This spacing-and-range structure is the most valuable feature of the paper for a
Thurstone-vs-Luce contest**, because it delivers pairs at controlled psychological distance. A Gumbel
(Luce) renormalization is scale-free in the odds; a Gaussian (Thurstone) renormalization is not, and
the divergence is a function of d'. Series A/B/C/D vary the range by factors of roughly 1:2:3:4.

Read these as a CRR failure profile, not a confirmation: **the 2x2 restrictions fail badly** — 28.6%,
21.4% and 50.0% of cells exceed Clarke's own .10 criterion for Duration, Intensity and Spacing D — and
the diagonal bias is **systematically positive** in 6 of 8 conditions, i.e. the CRR under-predicts
accuracy on small restricted sets. That is the signature of a renormalization that is too flat.

What is still missing and requires the original: **the matrices themselves**, without which no
zero-parameter forecast can be scored and no likelihood-ratio test computed.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Two-tier verdict.**

*Summary statistics: USABLE NOW.* All nine conditions' CRR deviation statistics are transcribed above
from the freely available Hodge (1967) PDF. Enough to state, with citation, that the auditory CRR
fails at 2x2 restriction depth and is biased in a consistent direction. Not enough for a distributional
forecasting contest.

*Matrices: NEEDS LIBRARY ACCESS, then digitizing.* Fourteen pages of 1962 JEP behind APA PsycNet.
Priority: **high, but below Clarke (1957), Egan (1957) and Pollack & Decker (1960a)** — because these
are tones rather than speech, and because the 8x8/4x4/2x2 structure is already partly characterised by
the summary statistics above. On the other hand it may be the single richest dataset in the branch if
the matrices are printed in full: nine 8x8 masters plus dozens of subset matrices, with spacing and
range under experimental control. If any one acquisition could support a full Thurstone-sigma vs
Luce-gamma contest with a discriminability axis, it is this one.

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

No abstract for this paper is available from any open source (APA deposits none, MEDLINE has none), so
I have no verbatim statement of conclusion from Hodge & Pollack themselves beyond the one fragment
quoted by Hodge (1967, p. 436) from p. 135 of the original:

> "...is independent of both the spacing within each series and the number of stimulus objects. The
> amount of transmitted information seems to be determined only by the range subtended by the stimulus
> series"

The authors' verdict as reported by Hodge five years later, verbatim from Hodge (1967, p. 429):

> "Hodge and Pollack (1962), however, using tones which varied in intensity or duration, obtained
> results in which the CRR did predict fairly adequately the confusion patterns, but with somewhat less
> precision than was the case with the multidimensional stimuli."

and, on the disagreement with Clarke, same page:

> "Although it might be argued that Clarke and Hodge and Pollack simply disagree about what constitutes
> acceptable CRR predictions, Clarke's view that the CRR will not work with single dimensional stimuli
> represents an important potential limitation of the rule's generality, and therefore deserves further
> testing."

Hodge's own later summary of the whole programme, verbatim from Hodge (1967) abstract:

> "The results demonstrated: (a) The CRR predicts equally well the response proportions of single
> dimensional visual, kinesthetic, and auditory stimulus ensembles, but less well than those for
> multidimensional auditory stimuli. (b) Better predictions are obtained with four than with two
> stimulus objects. (c) The CRR is sensitive to variations in the spacing and range of the stimulus
> ensembles and to practice on the task. It is concluded that the rule tends to fail whenever stimulus
> conditions elicit differential amounts of stimulus and response confusion."

Lee (1968, p. 219) on why the single/multi split arose, verbatim:

> "The results in Table I correspond to, and may help explain a generalization noted by Hodge and
> Pollack (1962) and Hodge (1967): the CRR does better for the multidimensional stimuli than for the
> unidimensional stimuli. There is nothing in Luce's choice theory to indicate such a result, nor did
> Clarke indicate any such expectation in his original presentation of CRR."
