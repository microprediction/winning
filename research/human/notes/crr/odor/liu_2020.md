# Liu et al. 2020 — SSomix: an 11-descriptor list instead of 4-AFC, but not nested

## Citation
Liu DT, Besser G, Lang M, Sharma G, Pablik E, Renner B, Mueller CA. "Odor Mixtures in Identification
Testing Using Sniffin' Sticks: The SSomix Test." *Scientific Reports* 2020;10(1):8155.
doi:10.1038/s41598-020-65028-7. PMC7234997. (Verified via Europe PMC core record.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction, cued identification of **odor mixtures**. Mixtures of two and three odors applied to a piece
of paper, built from **11 of the 16 items** of the original Sniffin' Sticks identification kit.
66 healthy subjects and 22 patients with olfactory dysfunction.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Set size does change — 4 → 11 — but it is NOT a nesting, and the stimulus changes too.**

Verified from the Methods, quoted:
> "Subjects were given a list with eleven descriptors sorted in alphabetical order at the beginning of
> the test and the instruction to place the piece of paper in front of both nostrils."
> "The task was to identify each individual odor of the odor mixtures from the list of eleven
> descriptors."

Two reasons it fails as a restriction test:
1. **Not nested.** The 11 descriptors are the 11 *target* names. The standard 4-AFC list for any given
   item contains distractors (blackberry, strawberry, grass…) that are not among those 11. So the
   standard 4-set is not a subset of the 11-set, nor vice versa — the two menus merely overlap.
2. **Stimulus differs.** SSomix presents 2- and 3-component *mixtures*, whereas the standard 4-AFC
   presents single odors. The odor being judged is not held fixed, so any share difference confounds
   menu size with mixture complexity.

Trap worth recording: "11 out of 16 items" in the abstract means eleven odor pens, and is easily
misread as an 11-alternative forced choice. It happens to be true that the response list has eleven
entries, but for a different reason.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Pooled psychometrics only; nothing per descriptor, nothing deposited.** The paper reports test–retest
reliability, validity, and ROC/AUC for discriminating patients from controls. No confusion matrix.

Data availability, verbatim:
> "The institutional ethics committee...is imposing legal and ethical restrictions on the present data.
> Requests for data will be administered by the corresponding author."

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable.** Three independent blockers: menus overlap rather than nest, the stimulus is mixtures
rather than single odors, and the data is ethics-restricted with no deposit. Small n (88) as well.

Logged so it is not re-investigated. It is the only olfactory study besides `negoias_2010.md` that
varies response-set cardinality at all, so it will keep surfacing in searches — the note is here to
short-circuit that.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
> "SSomix was a suitable downsizing of the original kit, especially regarding resource efficiency."

The authors also report "good to excellent test-retest reliability and validity" and that the AUC
"indicated good diagnostic accuracy in identifying patients with reduced and severely impaired
olfactory function." The eleven-descriptor list is presented purely as an administrative convenience —
it lets one list serve every mixture — and its consequences for choice behaviour are never discussed.
