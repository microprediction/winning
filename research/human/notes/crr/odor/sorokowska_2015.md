# Sorokowska et al. 2015 — extended 32-item Sniffin' Sticks: the 4-descriptor version of Negoias's odor set

## Citation
Sorokowska A, Albrecht E, Haehner A, Hummel T. "Extended version of the 'Sniffin' Sticks'
identification test: Test–retest reliability and validity." *Journal of Neuroscience Methods*
2015;251:56–64. PMID 25677404. doi:10.1016/j.jneumeth.2015.02.004.

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction, cued identification. The **32-item** extended Sniffin' Sticks identification subtest,
4-AFC — the same 32 odorants used in `negoias_2010.md`. Preliminary work in 46 normosmics to rate
intensity and familiarity of the added odors; main study 126 patients with olfactory loss and 71
normosmics, with retest in 69 controls at a mean interval of 4 days.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Fixed 4-alternative list per odor.** No manipulation within this paper.

Its value is as the **middle rung M(4)** of the chain implied by Negoias's construction:
T(3) ⊂ M(4) ⊂ S(6) over the same 32 odorants. Negoias's 3-AFC list is the standard 4-AFC list minus
one distractor, and his 6-AFC list is that same list plus two — so this paper defines the reference
menu both of his conditions were built from.

The fatal practical problem: the three menu sizes exist across **different samples** (Negoias's 238 in
Brussels; this paper's 197 in Dresden), and **no side deposited item-level data**. So the chain is
notional. It becomes real only if the Negoias raw sheets are recovered, or if someone re-runs all three
menus within subject.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Pooled reliability and validity statistics only.** Test–retest r = 0.88 for odor identification
(r = 0.80 discrimination, r = 0.92 threshold). No per-item choice data, no per-descriptor data, no
supplementary deposit.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable as data.** Nothing per-descriptor is printed, so there is nothing to digitize. Worth library
access only to obtain the 32-item descriptor lists in clean printed form — which would incidentally
help certify the `negoias_2010.md` Table 2 nesting list, since the two papers share the odorant set and
this one is the source of the reference 4-AFC menus.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
The conclusion is psychometric: the extended 32-item identification test is a **reliable and valid**
instrument suitable for repeated longitudinal testing of individual olfactory subfunctions, the point of
doubling the item count being to reduce measurement error enough to track change within a person.
Response-set size is not discussed.
