# Eluecque et al. 2015 — chance-corrected identification score in nasal polyposis

## Citation
Eluecque H, Nguyen DT, Jankowski R. "Influence of random answers on interpretation of the Sniffin' Stick
identification test in nasal polyposis." *European Annals of Otorhinolaryngology, Head and Neck Diseases*
2015;132(1):13–17. doi:10.1016/j.anorl.2013.07.002.

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction. Sniffin' Sticks 16-item identification, 4-AFC. 42 patients with nasal polyposis, tested
pre- and post-operatively.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Fixed 4 alternatives.** No manipulation. Conceptually adjacent to menu-size questions because it is
about the guessing floor that a 4-AFC format imposes — the authors subtract expected chance-correct
answers to form a "real identification score" (IR). Whether respondents could decline to answer beyond
the four descriptors is **not stated** in the record examined.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
Pooled scores and the derived IR statistic. **No per-descriptor data, no supplementary material, no
deposit.**

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
Europe PMC record fetched (EXT_ID 25482240); full text **paywalled** ("Subscription required"). No PMC
full text, no OA copy.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable.** Tiny n, no per-descriptor content, paywalled. Its only relevance is as a citation for the
point that forced-choice formats generate a guessing floor that varies with the number of alternatives —
which is the mechanism a menu-size analysis has to separate from genuine preference.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
> "Calculation of the IR identification score allows more precise interpretation of the results of the
> identification test in patients with severe anosmia."

Also reported: patients unable to detect n-butanol produced more random answers.
