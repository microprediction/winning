# Capkan & Yildirim 2025 — cued odor naming with open-ended responses (no fixed option sets)

## Citation
Capkan EN, Yildirim F. "Cues for odor naming affect performance and brain connectivity." *Frontiers in
Human Neuroscience* 2025;19:1671670. doi:10.3389/fnhum.2025.1671670. PMC12706689.

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction plus EEG/connectivity. Odor naming under two kinds of visual cue: **lexical cues** (letter
fragments of the odor name, e.g. "L- - -O -" for lemon) and **colour cues** (two representative colours
derived from image analysis of the odor source). Counterbalanced within subject.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Neither — there is no fixed response set at all.** Participants gave **open-ended verbal responses**,
later coded as correct / incorrect / no answer. So there is no menu, hence no nesting, overlapping, or
relabelling.

Recorded because a letter-fragment cue is arguably an implicit restriction of the response space (only
words matching the template are admissible), which is superficially menu-like. But the admissible set is
never enumerated for the participant and is not recoverable from the data, so it cannot be treated as a
choice set.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
Accuracy categories and connectivity measures, pooled. No response strings deposited.
Data availability, verbatim:
> "The raw data supporting the conclusions of this article will be made available by the authors, without
> undue reservation."

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable.** No choice sets exist to compare. Logged as a checked negative — it surfaces in searches for
"cued odor identification" but is a naming study, not a forced-choice one.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
The finding is that the *type* of cue changes both naming performance and brain connectivity — lexical
and colour cues are described as "semantically meaningful visual cues" that differentially support odor
retrieval. The relevance here is only that response format again shapes measured olfactory ability.
