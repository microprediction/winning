## Citation

Lacouture, Y., Li, S.-C., & Marley, A. A. J. (1998). The roles of stimulus and response
set size in the identification and categorisation of unidimensional stimuli. *Australian
Journal of Psychology*, 50(3), 165-174. DOI 10.1080/00049539808258793. Authors, volume,
issue and pagination confirmed via Crossref, and the citation is confirmed independently on
Shu-Chen Li's TU Dresden publication list.

## Domain and stimuli

Unidimensional visual stimuli (line lengths), identification and categorisation, with
both stimulus set size and response set size manipulated. This is the paper the whole
search was seeded from, and on design grounds it is the closest published match to what we
need.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Reported as nested, but I could not verify it from the source.** The design as
relayed to me: 10 stimuli held fixed while the number of response categories varies over
10, 5 and 2, using centrally located keys of one fixed response keyboard, giving nested
label sets {1..10} superset {3,4,5,6,7} superset {5,6}. A separate experiment nests stimuli
and responses together (sets of 10/8/6/4/2, middle stimuli with central keys), which is not
a pure response-set manipulation.

**Flagged unverified.** tandfonline.com returns HTTP 403 to every automated fetch I
attempted, so I have never seen this paper's text. The label scheme above came from a
delegated search thread that I could not independently confirm, and it is the single most
load-bearing unverified claim in this note set. Someone should open the PDF in a browser
and check (a) whether the reduced-response conditions really reuse the master labels rather
than relabelling to 1..N, and (b) whether the 10-to-5 and 10-to-2 mappings are contiguous
coarsenings or interleaved.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

**Reported to print condition-level means only** — probability correct and response
time per condition, with per-stimulus detail in figures rather than tables. If that is
right, no cell-level shares exist and the paper is unusable as a scoring target regardless
of how good the design is. Unverified, same caveat as above.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

Not read, so nothing quoted. Secondhand, via Lacouture & Marley (2004) which I did read:
that paper describes the 1998 work as having "successfully modeled the probabilities of
correct responses and the mean correct response times (RTs) in unidimensional absolute
identification tasks for various stimulus ranges and stimulus/response set sizes, for
individual and group data" and cites it for the extension "to the general case of
unidimensional categorization".

Also secondhand via Chris Kent's OSF registration `wzsrp`, which summarises the 1998 result
as: "Experiments using unidimensional stimuli in a categorization task have shown relatively
good performance compared to identification (Lacouture, Li, & Marley, 1998)... As the number
of response categories increases, performance decreases (Lacouture et al., 1998)."
