## Citation

Lee, W. (1970). Detection theory, micromatching, and the constant-ratio rule.
*Perception & Psychophysics*, 7(4). DOI 10.3758/BF03206305. University of California,
Berkeley. Theoretical paper, no new data.

## Domain and stimuli

No experiment. Calculations for sets of three stimuli and subsets of two, comparing what
signal-detection theory, "micromatching" (Lee 1963) and the CRR each imply about ratio
constancy.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested by construction, but analytically**: sets of three with subsets of two. No
empirical matrices.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

No data. The value here is (a) the analytic result that detection theory and the CRR
diverge most for univariate distributions, and (b) an explicit statement that the empirical
literature failed to publish cells.

**Unusable as data**, but directly relevant as prior art: it is the closest thing in this

contribution is positioned correctly against it.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

The analytic result, from the abstract:

> "The conformance of detection theory and micromatching with the constant-ratio rule (CRR)
> was determined by calculations for sets of three stimuli and subsets of two stimuli. For an
> arbitrary selection of sets, the CRR would be expected to hold quite well if either
> detection theory or micromatching lay behind the responses. For specific stimulus
> configurations, however, detection theory and micromatching differ considerably from one
> another and from the CRR. These configurations are found particularly for the univariate
> distributions, and this corresponds to empirical findings that the CRR holds better for
> multidimensional than for unidimensional stimuli. It is shown how the differences between
> the three theories in the implied constancies of ratios can be used in diagnosis of the
> basis of empirical confusion matrices."

That is, in 1970, the prediction that a Gaussian-race account and proportional
renormalization come apart exactly where the psychophysics said the CRR was failing — and the
proposal to use the gap diagnostically. This project is the empirical follow-through that
apparently never happened.

And the complaint that explains why so much of this branch is unusable:

> "I would suggest that investigators of the CRR give more detail on individual response
> probabilities for specific configurations, rather than simply giving gross plots and gross
> statistics. If this level of detail is not possible for a journal article, at least the
> availability of the data could be advertized."

## Correction (2026-08-18)

The year and volume above are wrong. The DOI recorded here, 10.3758/BF03206305, resolves at
Crossref to *Perception & Psychophysics* **4**(4):217-219, **1968** -- not 7(4), 1970. The
paper cites it as Lee (1968). The filename is left as `lee_1970.md` so links do not break.
