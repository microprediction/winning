## Citation

Dodds, P., Rae, B., & Brown, S. (2012). Perhaps unidimensional is not unidimensional.
*Cognitive Science*, 36(8), 1542-1555. DOI 10.1111/cogs.12003. Authors and pagination
confirmed via Crossref — note the third author is Scott Brown, not Donkin, which several
secondary sources get wrong.

## Domain and stimuli

Reanalysis of existing absolute-identification confusion data, arguing that nominally
unidimensional stimulus sets carry additional dimensions.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**No new response-set manipulation.** This is a reanalysis, so it inherits whatever
nesting the source experiments had — which, for the Newcastle datasets, is relabelling.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

No new data. Whether the reanalysis prints the confusion matrices it works from is
**unchecked** — it is the one thing that would make this paper valuable, since a
methodological reanalysis sometimes reproduces the input matrices in an appendix. Worth five
minutes.
