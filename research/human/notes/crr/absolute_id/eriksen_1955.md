## Citation

Eriksen, C. W., & Hake, H. W. (1955). Absolute judgments as a function of stimulus range
and number of stimulus and response categories. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 49,
323-332. DOI 10.1037/h0044211. Authors and pagination confirmed via Crossref.

Related, same authors: "Anchor effects in absolute judgments" (1956).

## Domain and stimuli

Absolute judgment, with stimulus range and the number of stimulus and response categories
manipulated. This is the earliest paper found that varies stimulus-category count and
response-category count as separate factors.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Unknown.** The title promises exactly the right factorisation — number of *stimulus*
categories and number of *response* categories varied independently, which is the design
Lacouture, Li & Marley revisited in 1998. Whether the response sets are nested over shared
labels is unverified. Not read.

Chris Kent's OSF registration `e58ha` cites it for the result that "manipulating the number
of stimuli whilst response set size remains constant has a minimal effect on performance
(Eriksen & Hake, 1955; Mori & Ward, 1995)" — which implies the two factors really were
crossed.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Needs library access.** Moderate priority, ranking just below Clarke 1957 and
Lacouture 1998. The crossed stimulus-count / response-count design is rare and would settle
whether the set-size effect lives on the response side, which is the premise the whole
branch rests on.
