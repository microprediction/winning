# Dalton et al. 2013 — NIH Toolbox Odor ID development: percent-correct only

## Citation
Dalton P, et al. "Olfactory assessment using the NIH Toolbox." *Neurology* 2013;80(11 Suppl 3):S32–6.
PMC3662337.

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction. Development and norming of the **NIH Toolbox Odor Identification Test**: 9 odorants, 4-AFC
with picture and word labels.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Fixed 4 alternatives per odor**, drawn from a shared pool of ~10 descriptors across the 9 items — the
same overlapping structure documented quantitatively in `jaen_2024.md`.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Percent correct per odorant, pooled.** No per-descriptor distributions, no deposit. Instrument manuals
and export dictionaries sit behind free registration at
https://resources.nihtoolbox.org/manuals-and-test-materials/nih-toolbox-documentation/.

Worth stating explicitly: the NIH Toolbox literature is percent-correct-only as a rule, and
`jaen_2024.md` is the **exception** that happens to carry chosen descriptors in a PLOS supplement. Do not
expect other Toolbox papers to be similar. ARMADA (Echevarria-Cooper et al. 2024, PMC10843554), which
evaluates the Toolbox odor test across normal cognition, MCI and AD, has **no data availability
statement** at all.

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3662337/ — **open access**.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable for choice shares** — aggregate percent correct only. Useful solely as the instrument
reference for `jaen_2024.md`.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified verbatim. The paper establishes the 9-item Toolbox odor identification measure as a brief,
validated olfactory assessment suitable for inclusion in a general neurological test battery.
