# Hunter et al. 2024 — SCENTinel, genetics, and printed distractor shares

## Citation
Hunter SR, Lin C, Nguyen H, Hannum ME, Bell K, Huang A, Joseph PV, Parma V, Dalton PH, Reed DR.
"Effects of genetics on odor perception: Can a quick smell test effectively screen everyone?"
*Chemical Senses* 2024;49:bjae025. doi:10.1093/chemse/bjae025. PMC11519045. (Verified via Europe PMC.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction. SCENTinel rapid test (see `parma_2021.md` for the instrument). Large remote sample;
analysis of how genotype affects perception of the specific odorants used, including which wrong
label people reach for.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Nested by inheritance** — this uses the SCENTinel 4-then-3 procedure, so T = S \ {first choice} on
the same odor and subject. The paper does not analyze the restriction; it reports first-attempt
choices only.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Pooled per-descriptor shares, printed in prose, for a small number of odors.** The clearest
example, verbatim: for those who misidentified banana, "the most selected distractor was flower
(34%)". This is a genuine choice share over the master set S, pooled across subjects — the only
SCENTinel number of that kind found in print.

No per-subject data, no complete 4-option distribution for any odor, no deposit. Same repository
situation as `parma_2021.md`: every advertised OSF link is private or PDF-only.

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11519045/ — **open access**.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Needs digitizing for the fragments; unusable for the full structure.**
The handful of prose percentages can be lifted directly, but they are incomplete (one distractor per
odor, not the full distribution) and pooled, so they cannot support a within-subject regularity test
on their own. Value here is corroborative: it proves Monell holds the distractor-level records and
that they are analyzable, strengthening the case for the data request in `parma_2021.md`.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
The paper's thrust is that genotype shapes which odors a screening test can rely on — the title asks
"Can a quick smell test effectively screen everyone?" and the answer is qualified: perception of the
specific odorants varies with genetics, so a single-odor screen is not uniformly valid across people.
The distractor observation ("the most selected distractor was flower (34%)") is offered as evidence
that misidentification is systematic rather than random, which is exactly the premise a choice-model
analysis would need.
