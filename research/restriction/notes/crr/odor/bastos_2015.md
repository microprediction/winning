# Bastos et al. 2015 — SS16-Child, binary data but a documented descriptor-substitution record

## Citation
Bastos LOD, et al. "Sniffin' Sticks 16-item test for children (SS16-Child)." *PLoS ONE*
2015;10(8):e0131641. (Author list beyond the first was not independently reconfirmed.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction in children. 16-item Sniffin' Sticks identification adapted for a paediatric sample, 4-AFC,
n = 51 children.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Fixed 4 alternatives.** Relevance is that the study **modified the descriptor lists** for child
comprehension, and unusually it *documents the substitutions* — S1 Table records which words were
replaced. That makes it a usable reference for constructing a relabelling comparison against the
standard adult lists, even though the study itself administered only one version.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Per-subject but binary.** Supplementary files S5 and S6 give 51 children × 16 items coded
"1 = correct, 0 = wrong". S1 Table (`...s002`) is the descriptor-substitution record.

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
Article and supplements are **open access** (PLOS, CC BY) via
https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0131641 with supplements at the
standard `article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0131641.s002` / `.s005` / `.s006` pattern.
Note: the supplement URLs were identified from the article record but were **not individually fetched**,
unlike the PLOS file in `jaen_2024.md`.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable for choice shares** (binary, n=51). **Usable as a descriptor-substitution reference** if a
relabelling design is ever built — S1 Table is the kind of documentation `delgado-losada_2021.md` and
the other cultural adaptations mostly fail to provide.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified verbatim. The paper validates a child-appropriate adaptation of the 16-item identification
test, concluding that descriptor substitution is necessary for paediatric use and that the adapted test
performs acceptably.
