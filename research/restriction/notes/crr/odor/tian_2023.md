# Tian et al. 2023 (BLSA) — 16-item Sniffin' Sticks, granularity publicly undeterminable

## Citation
Tian Q, et al. "Olfaction Is Related to Motor Function in Older Adults." (and related BLSA olfaction
papers) *Neurology* 2023;100:e964. Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging.
(Cited from the search record; the exact article title was not independently reconfirmed.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction. **Correction to a common assumption: BLSA uses the 16-item Sniffin' Sticks, not the B-SIT.**
That matters, because the Sniffin' Sticks data model *can* preserve the chosen descriptor
(see `nda_sniffin01.md`) whereas the B-SIT model cannot.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
Fixed 4-alternative list per item. No manipulation.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
Published work reports total scores. Whether per-item chosen descriptors are retained in the BLSA
database is **undeterminable from public sources** — no public variable list exists, and the BLSA
documentation page states staff cannot provide metadata before an application is approved.

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
https://blsa.nih.gov/documentation — **open** page, but contains no variable list. Data by application.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unknown, leaning unusable.** The only way to find out is to apply, and the application cannot be
informed in advance. Because BLSA uses Sniffin' Sticks rather than B-SIT it is the one epidemiological
cohort where per-descriptor retention is *plausible* — so worth one exploratory email, but ranked below
Monell, SNAC-K, and PPMI, all of which are known quantities.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified — the specific paper was not fetched. BLSA olfaction papers generally report associations
between total olfactory score and aging outcomes (motor function, cognition, mortality).
