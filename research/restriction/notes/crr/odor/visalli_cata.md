# Visalli et al. — free-comment versus supplied term list on identical aroma stimuli, open data

## Citation
Two Mendeley Data deposits from Visalli and colleagues (sensory science, Dijon):
1. https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/c7v4zj8vph/1 — 60 consumers free-comment vs 60 consumers CATA on
   the same 4 wines.
2. https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/fshtbhffth/1 — 436 consumers, 16 products, six temporal
   characterization methods including an explicit "list of terms vs. Free-Comment" contrast.
(Exact article citations and author lists were **not** independently verified; the deposits were located
via DataCite and their landing pages confirmed to resolve.)

## Domain and stimuli
Sensory/consumer science. Aroma and flavour characterization of wines (deposit 1) and 16 food products
(deposit 2) by consumers.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**A free (unbounded) master phase versus a supplied finite term list — the sensory-science analogue of the
SNAC-K free-then-cued design, and the only *open-data* instance of that contrast found anywhere.**

Critical limitation in deposit 1: the comparison is **between groups** (60 consumers free-comment, a
different 60 doing CATA), not within subject. So it does not satisfy "same subjects". Deposit 2 compares
six methods across 436 consumers; whether individuals did both a free and a listed condition on the same
products was not verified.

Also, as with `jaeger_2015.md`, these are check-all / free-text characterization tasks rather than
pick-one forced choice, so the choice object is a subset or a text string rather than a single
alternative.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
Both deposits are described as **raw, per-consumer, CC BY**. Landing pages confirmed to resolve
(HTTP 200); **file listings were not enumerated** in this session, so the exact granularity and column
structure remain to be checked.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Usable now, with a caveat that may be disqualifying.** Data is open and downloadable today — no
request, no paywall. But the free-vs-listed contrast in deposit 1 is between-subjects, which defeats the
main purpose. First action is cheap: download both, enumerate the files, and determine whether any
subject contributes both a free and a listed response for the same product. If deposit 2 does, it becomes
the only open dataset in this entire directory with a within-subject master/restricted pairing.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified — the corresponding articles were not fetched. The methodological literature this belongs to
generally concludes that supplied term lists and free description yield **different product
configurations**, with lists imposing the researcher's vocabulary while free comment captures consumer
vocabulary at the cost of comparability.
