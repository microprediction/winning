# Sulmont-Rossé et al. 2005 — free naming vs multiple choice on the same odors

## Citation
Sulmont-Rossé C, Issanchou S, Köster EP. "Odor Naming Methodology: Correct Identification with
Multiple-choice versus Repeatable Identification in a Free Task." *Chemical Senses* 2005;30(1):23–27.
doi:10.1093/chemse/bjh252. (Volume/pages/DOI verified via OpenAlex; author list beyond the first is
from the standard citation and was not independently reconfirmed.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction, odor naming methodology. Compares two response formats applied to the same odorants:
unconstrained free naming (scored on *repeatability* of the label a subject gives) versus
multiple-choice identification (scored on correctness).

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**A free master phase versus a small forced-choice list — the earliest explicit instance of the
contrast.** Master = the subject's own lexicon (unbounded); restricted = the printed multiple-choice
list. This is the methodological ancestor of the SNAC-K free-then-cued protocol
(`horberg_2025.md`, `lindroos_2022.md`).

Key difference from the SNAC-K design, and it matters: this paper's central point is that the two
formats are **not measuring the same thing on the same scale**, so treating the multiple-choice list as
a straightforward restriction of the free task is precisely what the authors caution against. Whether
the design is within-subject over identical odors (rather than between-groups) was not verified —
the paper is paywalled.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Unknown in detail; certainly nothing deposited (2005, pre-data-sharing).** No supplementary

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Needs library access, then probably needs digitizing.**
Low expected yield as data — n is likely small by modern standards and 2005 papers rarely print full
response distributions. High value as **framing**: it is the canonical citation for the claim that
free and cued identification are different tasks, which any argument built on a free→cued contraction
has to answer. Get the PDF; treat it as a conceptual reference rather than a data source.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified verbatim (paywalled). From the title and abstract framing: correct identification under
multiple choice and repeatable identification in a free task give **different pictures of odor naming
ability**, and the authors advocate repeatability in a free task as a measure that avoids the cueing
artifacts of a supplied list. The implication for this project is a warning: the supplied list does not
merely narrow the option set, it changes the retrieval problem.
