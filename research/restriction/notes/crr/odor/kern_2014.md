# Kern et al. 2014 — NSHAP olfaction: 4-AFC identification plus 3-AFC detection with an optional 4th

## Citation
Kern DW, Wroblewski KE, Schumm LP, Pinto JM, Chen RC, McClintock MK. "Olfactory function in Wave 2 of
the National Social Life, Health, and Aging Project." *The Journals of Gerontology Series B:
Psychological Sciences and Social Sciences* 2014;69 Suppl 2:S134–43. doi:10.1093/geronb/gbu093.
PMC4303099. (Verified via Europe PMC core record.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction, in a nationally representative US survey of older adults. NSHAP Waves 1–3. Two distinct
tasks, both administered with Sniffin'-Sticks-style felt-tip pens by field interviewers:
- **Identification** ("blue pen"): 5 odorants, 4-AFC.
- **Detection** ("red pen" n-butanol at 6 concentrations; "green pen" androstadienone at 4).

Correction to a common assumption: the identification task is **5 odorants × 4 alternatives**, not
5-AFC.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
Two separate answers.

**Identification: no restriction.** Fixed 4-alternative printed list per odor:
- BLUEPEN_1 rose → {chamomile, raspberry, rose, cherry}
- BLUEPEN_2 leather → {smoke, glue, leather, grass}
- BLUEPEN_3 orange → {orange, blueberry, strawberry, onion}
- BLUEPEN_4 fish → {bread, fish, cheese, ham}
- BLUEPEN_5 peppermint → {chive, peppermint, pine, onion}

**Detection: a naturally occurring menu augmentation, and the more interesting half.** The
`REDPEN2_1`–`_6` and `GREENPEN2_1`–`_4` variables are genuine 3-alternative choices — "which of the
three pens has the odor" — with the **correct pen's position stated in the variable label** (e.g.
"n-butanol 8.0% (pen 3)"). Each is coded `one / two / three / none of the pens have the odor (if
volunteered)`. So the master set is {pen1, pen2, pen3, none} and the *intended* set is
{pen1, pen2, pen3}, with the fourth option available only if the respondent volunteers it — an
asymmetric, interviewer-mediated set augmentation over ~10 trials per respondent with a known correct
alternative. That is closer to a menu manipulation than anything else in the public survey data, though
the augmentation is not randomized and its availability is confounded with respondent assertiveness.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Per-subject, chosen alternative, in the public-use files, all three waves.**
Variables: `BLUEPEN_1`–`BLUEPEN_5`, `BLUEPEN_FLAG`, `SML_INTRO`; `REDPEN2_1`–`_6`;
`GREENPEN2_1`–`_4`. Studies: ICPSR 20541 (Wave 1), 34921 (Wave 2), 36873 (Wave 3), distributed via
ICPSR/NACDA. The paper itself reports pooled prevalence and psychometrics, not per-descriptor shares.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Usable after a free registration.** Nothing to digitize. Only 5 identification items, so the
identification half is thin next to `nhanes_2014.md` — but it is nationally representative with three
waves of longitudinal follow-up, and the detection trials are unique in offering a known-correct
3-alternative choice with an optional fourth. Worth pulling mainly for the detection variables.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
The paper is a measurement report establishing that olfactory function can be validly assessed in a
national in-home survey, documenting the psychometrics of the Wave 2 protocol and providing prevalence
estimates of olfactory dysfunction in older US adults. It makes no claim about response alternatives;
the option structure is documented in the instrument and codebook rather than argued about.
