# Ribeiro et al. 2016 — European Portuguese Sniffin' Sticks: the deposit DOI is dead

## Citation
Ribeiro JC, et al. "Cultural Adaptation of the Portuguese Version of the 'Sniffin' Sticks' Smell Test:
Reliability, Validity, and Normative Data." *PLoS ONE* 2016;11(2):e0148937. PMC4749276.

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction. European Portuguese adaptation of the Sniffin' Sticks battery including the 16-item
identification subtest, 4-AFC.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**Fixed 4 alternatives.** A relabelling relative to the German original (culturally adapted descriptors),
but only one version administered.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
The paper states data are deposited at figshare `10.6084/m9.figshare.2007474`. **That deposit does not
exist.** Confirmed three independent ways:
1. DOI resolver → HTTP 404.
2. figshare API `/v2/articles/2007474` → `{"message": "Entity not found: ArticleVersion"}`.
3. Title search on figshare returns only figure objects 1644293–1644297, not a dataset.

So the published data availability statement is broken and the underlying data is not obtainable.

## Access (a DIRECT url you fetched; open, paywalled, or Wayback-only)
Paper: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4749276/ — **open access**.
Data: **dead.** No Wayback capture of the figshare record was located either.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)
**Unusable — the deposit is gone.** Recorded precisely so this is not chased again: the citation looks
like an open dataset and is not one. If the Portuguese data mattered it would need an author email, with
the caveat that even the original deposit's granularity is unknown.

A standing lesson for our own practice: this is what a revocable or unmaintained deposit looks like ten
years on — directly relevant to the archiving warning in `tolomeo_2026.md`.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
Not verified verbatim. The paper reports reliability, validity and Portuguese normative data for the
adapted battery, concluding it is suitable for clinical use in Portugal.
