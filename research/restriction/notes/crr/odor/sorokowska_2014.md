# Sorokowska et al. 2014 — presentation order of labels vs odor (menu size held fixed)

## Citation
Sorokowska A, Albrecht E, Hummel T. "Reading first or smelling first? Effects of presentation order on
odor identification." *Attention, Perception & Psychophysics* 2015;77(3):731–736.
doi:10.3758/s13414-014-0811-3. PMC4381111. (Published online 2014; journal volume dated 2015.)

## Domain and stimuli
Olfaction, cued identification. Sniffin' Sticks identification, 4-AFC throughout. Within-subject
manipulation of the *order* in which the labels and the odor are encountered.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)
**No restriction — four alternatives in every condition. What varies is timing, not the menu.**

Two conditions, verbatim:
- reading-first: subjects "read four provided options (the label of a target odor and three
  distractors), then they smelled an odor, and afterward they selected an answer"
- smelling-first: "the subject smelled an odor first, read provided labels afterward, and then selected
  an answer"

Recorded here because it is the nearest neighbour to a menu manipulation in the Sniffin' Sticks
literature and will surface in the same searches. It is genuinely a different manipulation: the option
set is identical in both arms, so it speaks to whether labels prime perception, not to whether choice
depends on the option set. Useful as evidence that *procedural* details move identification rates —
relevant background for arguing that menu composition might too.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)
**Pooled accuracy per condition only.** No per-descriptor choice data. No data availability statement,
no supplementary material.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible
The finding is that presentation order **does** affect identification performance — encountering the
verbal labels before versus after the odor changes accuracy — which the authors attribute to labels
shaping the perceptual/semantic search. Not verified verbatim beyond the methods quotes above. The
takeaway for this project is corroborative rather than substantive: identification is demonstrably
sensitive to how the response options are presented, independent of what they are.
