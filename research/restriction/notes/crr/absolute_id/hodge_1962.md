## Citation

Hodge, M. H., & Pollack, I. (1962). Confusion matrix analysis of single and
multidimensional auditory displays. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 63, 129-142.
DOI 10.1037/h0042219. Authors and pagination confirmed via Crossref.

## Domain and stimuli

Auditory displays, tones varying in intensity or duration (single-dimensional) and
multidimensional auditory stimuli. Includes a spacing/range manipulation — Hodge (1967)
refers back to "Hodge & Pollack, 1962, Experiment VII" for interstimulus spacing and
ensemble range effects.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested, per Hodge's own later description.** This is the direct predecessor to Hodge
(1967) and applies the CRR to master matrices and submatrices in the same way. Unverified in
the primary source.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

Unknown. Given that Hodge (1967) printed only summary statistics, and that Wayne Lee
(1970) complained about the whole CRR literature "simply giving gross plots and gross
statistics", the prior is not good. Worth checking anyway, because a 14-page JEP paper titled
"Confusion matrix analysis" is the most likely place in this literature to have printed
actual matrices.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Needs library access.** Check alongside Clarke 1957. Distinctive value: it is
unidimensional *auditory*, and it is the source of the finding that the CRR does better on
multidimensional than unidimensional stimuli — the residual we care about, in the modality
where it was first localised.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

Not read. Secondhand via Hodge (1967): "Hodge and Pollack (1962), however, using tones
which varied in intensity or duration, obtained results in which the CRR did predict fairly
adequately the confusion patterns, but with somewhat less precision than was the case with
the multidimensional stimuli."

That is another instance of approximately-right-with-a-systematic-residual, and via Lee
(1970): "Hodge and Pollack (1962), e.g., differentiated cases in which the CRR was more, or
less apt to hold. Multidimensional stimuli seemed to favor the CRR more than unidimensional
ones."
