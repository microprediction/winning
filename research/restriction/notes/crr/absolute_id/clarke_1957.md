## Citation

Clarke, F. R. (1957). Constant-ratio rule for confusion matrices in speech
communication. *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, 29(6), 715-720.
DOI 10.1121/1.1909023.

Companion and follow-ups, all located but none read:
- Clarke, F. R. (1957). Confusion matrices and the constant-ratio rule. *JASA*, 29, 781. DOI 10.1121/1.1918911.
- Clarke, F. R., & Anderson, C. D. (1957). Further test of the constant-ratio rule in speech communication. *JASA*, 29, 1318-1320. DOI 10.1121/1.1908778.
- Clarke, F. R. (1959). Proportion of correct responses as a function of the number of stimulus-response alternatives. *JASA*, 31, 835. (Cited in Hodge 1967's reference list; this is the set-size paper, and the one where Clarke reported the rule failing for unidimensional stimuli.)

## Domain and stimuli

Speech identification in noise. Spoken initial English consonants, consonant-vowel
syllables, words and numbers, across a range of signal-to-noise ratios.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Genuinely nested — this is the origin of the whole design.** Secondary sources
consistently describe master matrices with submatrices drawn from them: three 6x6 master
matrices for CVs with six 3x3 submatrices, and an 8x8 with three 4x4s. The rule itself is
the nesting hypothesis: the ratio between any two entries in a row of a submatrix equals the
ratio between the corresponding entries of the master matrix, assuming "the only variables
which differ systematically in obtaining the two matrices are the different sets of messages
and the allowable responses."

Unverified in the primary source — see Access.

## What numbers are printed or deposited (which tables/files, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

Unknown. Wayne Lee (1970), who did read it, says "Clarke (1957) published results
utilizing spoken syllables, words, and numbers, with highly favorable results", and Luce
(1959) "took these results as the only findings bearing on the adequacy of his axiom I".
Whether the matrices themselves are printed, or only the deviation summaries, I could not
determine. The often-quoted figure — average deviation about four percent between observed
4x4 consonant confusions and those predicted from the 8x8 — is a summary statistic, which is
mildly discouraging.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

Not read, nothing quoted directly. The rule as restated by Townsend & Landon (1982), who
had the paper in front of them: "the ratio between any two entries in a row of the subset
matrix is equal to the ratio between the corresponding two entries in the master matrix."

Hodge (1967) reports Clarke's own later retreat: "In an experiment with simple auditory
stimuli, e.g., tones varying in frequency or intensity, Clarke (1959) noted that the rule
tended to fail. In Clarke's opinion, the rule failed because the ordering inherent in single
dimensional stimuli produces contextual constraints or biases which, by definition, are
incompatible with the CRR."
