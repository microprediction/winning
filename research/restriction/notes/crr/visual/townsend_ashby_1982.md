# Townsend & Ashby (1982) — DOES NOT QUALIFY (near miss: payoffs, not response sets)

## Citation

Townsend, J. T., & Ashby, F. G. (1982). Experimental test of contemporary mathematical models
of visual letter recognition.
*Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*, 8(6), 834–864.
(Purdue University; Ohio State University. NIMH Grant 1R03MH28551-01, NSF Grant 7920298.)

This is the study cited as "Note 2" throughout Townsend & Landon (1982) — the source of the
claim that A/H and E/F are the highly similar pairs in the block-letter font, and the study
whose stimulus set Townsend & Landon deliberately reused: "the stimulus set selected for the
present study was chosen on the basis of being able to provide a straightforward manipulation
of set context and size as well as being identical in form to a previous study (Townsend &
Ashby, Note 2)."

Included in this directory because it is the closest sibling of the one fully usable
visual CRR dataset, and because "manipulated payoffs across the four stimulus letters" reads,
at a glance, like an alternative-set manipulation. It is not.

## Stimuli and master response set

**Four block capital letters constructed from line segments of equal length**, presented at
brief durations — the same equal-segment font later used with five letters (A, E, F, H, X) by
Townsend & Landon (1982). From the abstract:

> "A letter confusion experiment that used brief durations manipulated payoffs across the four
> stimulus letters, which were composed of line segments equal in length."

A distinctive extra measurement: observers reported the **features** they perceived as well as
the letter, allowing the sensory sampling stage to be separated from the letter-decision stage.

> "The observers were required to report the features they perceived as well as to give a letter
> response."

**Master response set = the four letters, throughout.**

## Restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**NONE. The manipulation is PAYOFFS, not the alternative set.**

Payoff manipulation shifts response **bias** (β in the similarity-choice model) while leaving
the set of available alternatives at four in every condition. The CRR/IIA question is what
happens when an alternative is *withdrawn*; a payoff shift is the orthogonal manipulation, and
in fact the one the CRR is *least* threatened by, since Luce's model absorbs it entirely into
the bias vector.

This makes the paper a genuine near miss rather than a candidate: same author, same font, same
paradigm, same laboratory, four letters instead of five — but the wrong knob turned. Townsend &
Landon (1982) is precisely this study rerun with the alternative set as the independent
variable, and it is the reason they could assert that no suitable data existed beforehand.

## What numbers are printed (which tables, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

Not exhaustively catalogued here, since the paper is disqualified on design and the PDF is
image-only. From the abstract, the reported quantities are **4 × 4 letter confusion matrices**
plus **feature-report frequencies**, fitted by four substantive feature-processing models and
three descriptive models:

> "The early feature-sampling process is separated from the later letter-decision process in the
> substantive feature models, and predictions are thus obtained for the frequencies of feature
> report as well as letter report. Four substantive visual feature-processing models are
> developed and tested against one another and against three models of a more descriptive
> nature."

Any confusion matrices are 4 × 4 and are therefore small; whether they are printed per subject
or pooled was not established, because the PDF carries no text layer and the question does not
affect the exclusion.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**UNUSABLE for a CRR test — excluded on design (payoff manipulation, fixed 4-alternative
menu).**

Secondary value, in priority order behind everything else in this directory:

- The **payoff manipulation is itself a clean test of a different invariance**: does a bias
  shift leave the similarity structure alone, as the Luce family requires? Townsend & Landon
  found that shrinking the response set moved η systematically (up on 69% of comparisons) while
  β "showed no systematic variation." The mirror-image experiment — move β deliberately via
  payoffs, and check whether η stays put — is exactly what this paper contains. If η is
  invariant under payoff shifts but not under set restriction, that localises the failure
  sharply, and it is an argument a Thurstonian account has to make.
- The **feature reports** provide an external, non-circular handle on the similarity structure,
  independent of the confusion matrix being modelled.

Both would require digitizing an image-only 31-page scan, and neither is needed for the primary
question. Queue behind Townsend & Landon (1982), Getty et al. (1979) and Townsend (1971b).

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

**The constant-ratio rule is not the subject of this paper** and no CRR test is reported. The
verdict recorded in the abstract is a within-set model comparison, and it goes to the *least
constrained* Luce-family model — the same outcome Townsend & Landon reached, and the same reason
the constrained version was worth testing:

> "The substantive models predict the decisional letter report phase much better than they do
> the feature-sampling phase, but the best overall 4 × 4 letter confusion matrix fits are
> obtained with one of the descriptive models, the similarity choice model."

The two findings that undercut the mechanistic feature accounts:

> "The present and other recent results suggest that the assumption that features are sampled in
> a stochastically independent manner may not be generally valid. The traditional high-threshold
> conceptualization of feature sampling is also falsified by the frequent reporting by observers
> of features not contained in the stimulus letter."

The relevance to the present project is indirect but real: the similarity-choice model wins on
*fit within a set*, every time, in every paper in this literature — and that is compatible with
its failing on *forecast across sets*, which is the only place a zero-parameter comparison
between Gumbel and Gaussian can be made. Townsend & Landon (1982) is where that distinction is
drawn, and Townsend & Ashby (1982) is the within-set half of the same programme.
