# Hodge, Piercy & Crawford (1961) — and the sibling USAF ESD reports

## Citation

Hodge, M.H., Piercy, M.L., & Crawford, M.J. (1961). *The constant-ratio rule and lifted
weights*. USAF Electronic Systems Division, ESD-TN-61-61, June 1961. DTIC accession
AD0264314. No DOI. OpenAlex work W283862519.

Two sibling reports from the same programme, cited in the same reference lists, are covered
here because they are the same first author, the same year and only obtainable together:

- Hodge, M.H., Crawford, M.J., & Piercy, M.L. (1961). *The constant-ratio rule and visual
  displays*. USAF: ESD-TDR-61-56, December 1961. (Also indexed as PsycEXTRA
  10.1037/e465712004-001.)
- Hodge, M.H. (1962). *The constant-ratio rule and identification tasks*. USAF: ESD-TDR-62-2,
  January 1962. (Also indexed as PsycEXTRA 10.1037/e422892004-001.)

The published consolidation of this programme is Hodge (1967), *Perception & Psychophysics*
2, 429–437 — see hodge1967.md, which is the version to cite and the version worth chasing.
The closely related journal paper is Hodge, M.H., & Pollack, I. (1962). Confusion matrix
analysis of single and multidimensional auditory displays. *Journal of Experimental
Psychology*, 63, 129–142.

## Domain and stimuli

**Lifted weights** (ESD-TN-61-61): kinesthetic magnitude identification. Subjects lift and
identify members of a weight ensemble.

**Visual displays** (ESD-TDR-61-56): visual magnitude identification — size and brightness,
per the Hodge (1967) consolidation.

**Identification tasks** (ESD-TDR-62-2): the general absolute-identification framing;
Engstrand & Moeller (1967) cite this report specifically for the finding that CRR accuracy
depends on **inter-stimulus spacing**.

Non-speech, non-letter. Lifted weights is the only pre-2000 CRR test in the kinesthetic /
weight-judgment domain that I could locate anywhere in this sweep.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

Nested ensemble sizes. In the Hodge (1967) consolidation the ensembles are 2, 4 or 8
objects on a single dimension, and the CRR is used to predict the smaller-ensemble response
proportions from the larger-ensemble confusion matrix by proportional renormalization over
the surviving rows and columns. The reports themselves have not been read, so the exact
ensemble sizes and whether the subsets were run on the same subjects cannot be confirmed
here.

Engstrand & Moeller (1967) attribute specific CRR failure modes to specific reports in this
series, which tells you what each report manipulated:

- ESD-TDR-62-2 (Hodge 1962): inter-stimulus **spacing**.
- ESD-TN-61-61 (Hodge, Piercy & Crawford 1961, lifted weights): level of **task difficulty**
  and **practice effects**.
- ESD-TDR-61-56 (Hodge, Crawford & Piercy 1961, visual displays): **response-response
  confusions**.

## What numbers are printed (which tables, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

Unknown — the reports have not been retrieved. USAF ESD technical notes of this era normally
print the full confusion matrices as raw frequency tables plus the CRR-derived submatrices
side by side, which is why they are worth the effort, but this is an expectation, not a
verified fact.

Hodge & Pollack (1962, *JEP* 63, 129–142) — the auditory-display companion — does print
matrices and is the easier proxy if the reports cannot be got.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Needs library access — specifically, a DTIC request once the site is back up.** The
documents are US government technical reports, unclassified and distribution-unlimited by
this vintage, so they should be free once DTIC is reachable; retry
`https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/citations/AD0264314` and the `sti/tr/pdf/AD0264314.pdf` endpoint.
NTIS is the fallback supplier, and a large US research library with a USAF/ESD report series
holding (or the Hodge papers at the University of Georgia) is the last resort.

Do not spend effort here before getting **Hodge (1967)**, which reports the same five
experiments in a peer-reviewed form and is a single Springer paywall away. Fetch the reports
only if the 1967 paper turns out not to print the matrices.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

No verbatim text available — the reports were not retrieved and no abstract for any of the
three surfaced in OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, or the Internet Archive.

The programme's conclusion is on record in the consolidation, Hodge (1967), abstract:

> "It is concluded that the rule tends to fail whenever stimulus conditions elicit
> differential amounts of stimulus and response confusion."

And, secondhand but precise, Engstrand & Moeller (1967, *Human Factors* 9, 445) summarising
which report showed what:

> "Hodge and his colleagues attributed weaknesses in prediction to inter-stimulus spacing
> (Hodge, 1962), level of task difficulty (Hodge, Piercy, and Crawford, 1961),
> response-response confusions (Hodge, Crawford, and Piercy, 1961), and practice effects
> (Hodge, Piercy, and Crawford, 1961)."
