# Hodge (1967)

## Citation

Hodge, M.H. (1967). Some further tests of the constant-ratio rule. *Perception &
Psychophysics*, 2(10), 429–437. DOI 10.3758/BF03208790. Published October 1967.
(Milton H. Hodge, University of Georgia; earlier work done for USAF Electronic Systems
Division — see hodge1961.md.)

This is the journal consolidation of the USAF ESD report series and is **the single most
on-target non-speech CRR paper in the weight / magnitude-judgment domain**.

## Domain and stimuli

Absolute identification of single-dimensional magnitude ensembles across three modalities:

- **Kinesthetic / weight**: lifted weights.
- **Visual**: size, and separately brightness.
- **Auditory**: single-dimensional ensembles (and, by comparison with earlier work, the
  multidimensional auditory displays of Hodge & Pollack, 1962).

Five experiments. Stimulus ensembles of **2, 4, or 8 objects** on one dimension. Subject's
task on each trial is to identify which member of the ensemble was presented. The spacing
and range of the ensembles were also varied, and practice effects were tracked.

Non-speech and non-letter throughout. This is exactly the "weight and magnitude judgment"
target.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

Nested by construction: the ensemble size is the manipulated variable (2, 4, 8), and the
CRR is the rule that predicts the smaller-ensemble response proportions from the
larger-ensemble confusion matrix by proportional renormalization over the surviving rows
and columns. In Hodge's framing (following Clarke 1957) the 8-object matrix is the master
matrix and the 4- and 2-object matrices are the submatrices to be predicted.

The abstract distinguishes two findings that matter for this project: the CRR predicted
single-dimensional visual, kinesthetic and auditory data about equally well, but *less*
well than multidimensional auditory data; and predictions were **better with four than with
two** stimulus objects — i.e. the rule degrades as the subset shrinks, which is the same
overprediction-with-shrinking-subset pattern Engstrand & Moeller (1967) report (see
engstrand1967.md).

Whether the restricted sets were run as separate blocks with separate subject groups, or as
nested subsets of one ensemble, cannot be settled from the abstract alone — the full text is
needed.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Needs library access.** The abstract alone is enough to establish the result and cite it,
but not enough to refit anything: no matrices, no counts, no subject numbers are available.
Springer paywalls it and there is no green-OA or Wayback copy. Get it through an
institutional Springer subscription (Perception & Psychophysics 1967 is in the Springer
back-file) or via interlibrary loan; then check whether the confusion matrices for the
2/4/8-object weight and brightness ensembles are printed. If they are, this becomes a
first-rate dataset: a single-author, single-lab, three-modality, three-ensemble-size CRR
battery with weight included.

Priority: **high**. Weight/magnitude judgment is otherwise a gap in the CRR literature, and
this paper's conclusion is a failure condition for the rule stated in mechanistic terms.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

Full abstract, verbatim:

> "Five experiments sought to test the constant-ratio rule (CRR) with single dimensional
> ensembles composed of 2, 4, or 8 stimulus objects. Each S attempted to identify stimuli
> which varied in weight or in visual size or brightness. The results demonstrated: (a) The
> CRR predicts equally well the response proportions of single dimensional visual,
> kinesthetic, and auditory stimulus ensembles, but less well than those for
> multidimensional auditory stimuli. (b) Better predictions are obtained with four than with
> two stimulus objects. (c) The CRR is sensitive to variations in the spacing and range of
> the stimulus ensembles and to practice on the task. It is concluded that the rule tends to
> fail whenever stimulus conditions elicit differential amounts of stimulus and response
> confusion."

The last sentence is the load-bearing one: the CRR fails **whenever the manipulation changes
the balance of stimulus versus response confusion**, which is a statement that context
changes the underlying representation, not just the normalization. Engstrand & Moeller
(1967) summarise Hodge's programme as attributing the failures to "inter-stimulus spacing
(Hodge, 1962), level of task difficulty (Hodge, Piercy, and Crawford, 1961),
response-response confusions (Hodge, Crawford, and Piercy, 1961), and practice effects
(Hodge, Piercy, and Crawford, 1961)."
