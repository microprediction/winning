# Leonard (1959)

## Citation

Leonard, J.A. (1959). Tactual choice reactions: I. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental
Psychology*, 11(2), 76–83. DOI 10.1080/17470215908416294. Published May 1959. Applied
Psychology Research Unit / University of Cambridge (Psychological Abstracts gives
"U. Cambridge"; OpenAlex gives the APRU affiliation).

The "Part I" in the title implies a sequel; none was located under a matching title.

## Domain and stimuli

**Tactile / vibrotactile identification of stimulated fingers.** Stimuli were delivered as
vibrations of relay armatures applied to the fingers, one finger stimulated per trial, with
**1, 2, 4, or 8 fingers** in the active set. The response was to depress the armature with
the very finger that had been stimulated — a maximally compatible, ideomotor
stimulus-response mapping, which is the whole point of the experiment.

Non-speech, non-letter, and the only tactile study in this sweep that varies set size over
the classic 1/2/4/8 series.

The dependent variable is **choice reaction time**, not identification accuracy. The paper is
a landmark in the Hick–Hyman literature precisely because it found the information-theoretic
RT law to fail under high S-R compatibility.

## Master response set and restricted response sets (nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

Nested by construction, in the strongest physical sense available: the response set is the set
of stimulated fingers, of size 1, 2, 4, or 8, and each smaller set is literally a subset of
the fingers used in the larger. Stimulus set and response set are identical and move together
— you cannot restrict the menu without also removing the stimulus, because the "menu" is which
fingers are in play.

That identity is a problem, not a feature, for CRR purposes. A constant-ratio test needs the
odds between two *surviving* alternatives, i.e. the same stimulus presented under two
different menus. Here, reducing 8 fingers to 2 changes which stimuli can occur, so there is no
condition in which a fixed stimulus is judged against both a large and a small response set.
The design is a set-size manipulation of the Hick type, not a Clarke-type master-matrix /
submatrix manipulation.

## What numbers are printed (which tables, counts or proportions, per subject or pooled)

**Not established, and probably no confusion matrices at all.** The retrievable summary
reports only reaction times: a difference between simple RT (1 alternative) and two-choice RT,
and no systematic differences among 2-, 4-, and 8-choice RTs. Nothing about error rates or
misidentifications is mentioned.

Under a maximally compatible mapping — respond with the finger that buzzed — error rates would
be near floor, which is the standard reason this class of experiment reports no confusion
matrix. Any tactile confusion structure would be swamped.

Whether error counts appear anywhere in the eight printed pages cannot be settled without the
article.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**Effectively unusable for a constant-ratio test, and it should not be prioritised.** Two
independent reasons, either of which is fatal:

1. The dependent variable is reaction time. There is no reported confusion matrix and, given
   the deliberately maximal S-R compatibility, almost certainly no usable error structure to
   build one from.
2. Stimulus set and response set are the same object, so no stimulus is ever judged under two
   different menus. Even with perfect error data you could not form the odds ratio the CRR
   constrains.

If someone wants to close the loop anyway, the paper is one PDF away behind any SAGE or
Taylor & Francis QJEP back-file subscription — cheap to check, and worth five minutes to
confirm whether error counts were tabulated. But the tactile-domain need for this project is
better served by **Hodge (1967)**, whose kinesthetic (lifted-weight) ensembles of 2, 4 and 8
objects are a genuine identification task with real confusions, and by the tactile-aid
identification literature (e.g. Reed's Tadoma analyses) if a true tactile confusion matrix is
required.

Recorded here mainly so the citation is not left as an unchecked lead: Leonard (1959) is
frequently invoked for the 1/2/4/8 set-size series, and it is worth having on file that the
series is an RT series, not a confusion series.

## What the authors concluded, quoted verbatim where possible

The article itself has not been read, so no sentence of Leonard's own prose can be quoted
directly. The *Psychological Abstracts* entry does, however, quote the paper internally — the
material inside its quotation marks is Leonard's or close paraphrase of it.

Full entry, verbatim from the OCR (the stray "A_" is a scanning artefact):

> "2560. Leonard, J. Alfred. (U. Cambridge) Tactual choice reactions. Part I. Quart. J. exp.
> Psychol., 1959(May), 11, 76-83.—A study of the effect of 'varying the number of alternatives
> on choice reaction time in a task having an initial high degree of compatibility. The stimuli
> were presented as vibrations of relay armatures to 1, 2, 4, or 8 fingers separately, and the
> response was to depress the armature by the finger so stimulated. . . . results showed a
> difference between simple reaction time and two-choice times, but no systematic differences
> among 2, 4, or 8 choice times.'—M. J. Wayner, Jr."

The substantive conclusion, then, is that under a highly compatible mapping **choice reaction
time is flat from 2 to 8 alternatives** — the number of available alternatives stops mattering
once the mapping is direct. That is a genuinely interesting adjacent result (it is evidence
against a decision stage whose cost scales with menu size) but it is a claim about latency,
not about the invariance of choice odds, and it should not be cited as a CRR test.
