# Fantino & Dunn 1983 — delay-reduction hypothesis extended to three-alternative choice

## Citation

Fantino, E. & Dunn, R. M. (1983). The delay-reduction hypothesis: Extension to three-alternative choice. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Animal Behavior Processes*, 9(2), 132–146. doi:10.1037/0097-7403.9.2.132. Published April 1983.

Bibliographic details verified three ways: Crossref (fetched, gives authors Edmund Fantino and Roger M. Dunn, vol 9 issue 2, pp. 132–146, issued 1983-04); the reference list of Jacob & Fantino (1988, p. 380); and the reference list of Fantino & Davison (1983, p. 12).

**Do not confuse with a different 1983 Fantino paper**: Fantino, E. & Davison, M. (1983), "Choice: some quantitative relations", *JEAB* 40(1), 1–13, doi:10.1901/jeab.1983.40-1 — that one is two-alternative concurrent chains throughout and is not a restricted-set study; it has no note file. A third, related item is a conference paper: Fantino, E., Abarca, N. & Dunn, R., "The delay-reduction hypothesis: Extensions to foraging and three-alternative choice", Fifth Harvard Symposium on Quantitative Analyses of Behavior, Cambridge MA, 1982 — cited as Reference Note 1 in Fantino & Davison (1983).

## Domain and stimuli

**Animal subjects on concurrent-chains schedules — pigeons.** Not a human confusion-matrix dataset. The alternatives are response keys in the initial links of concurrent-chains schedules; responses in an initial link intermittently produce entry into a terminal link (a stimulus correlated with a delay to food). Choice is measured as the distribution of initial-link responses. The theoretical variable is *delay reduction* — the improvement in time-to-food signalled by terminal-link onset — not stimulus similarity.

Subject counts and exact schedule values are NOT OBTAINED (see Access below).

## Master and restricted response sets

This is the deletion design, on the strength of two independent first-hand descriptions by Fantino himself:

- **RESTRICTED menu of 2 alternatives:** a two-key concurrent-chains schedule.
- **MASTER menu of 3 alternatives:** the same procedure changed to a three-key concurrent-chains schedule.

Jacob & Fantino (1988, p. 368) state that Fantino & Dunn "conducted three experiments in which a two-key concurrent-chains schedule was changed to a three-key concurrent-chains schedule." Fantino & Davison (1983, p. 12) describe the same work as assessing "the applicability of the delay-reduction hypothesis to choice for three VI schedules" and report a summary statistic "averaged over four experiments" — so the two secondary sources disagree on whether there were three or four experiments. That discrepancy is unresolved here and may reflect the 1982 Harvard Symposium paper being counted in or out.

The predictions being contrasted, as set out in Jacob & Fantino (1988, p. 368): "When a third alternative chain is added, the delay-reduction hypothesis predicts that preference for the original alternatives will (a) diverge when the added alternative provides the highest rate of reinforcement, (b) converge when the added alternative provides a sufficiently lower rate of reinforcement than the original alternatives, and (c) not change when the addition of the third alternative does not change the overall delay to reinforcement in the situation. Thus, only in the latter case do the delay-reduction hypothesis and the constant-ratio rule make the same prediction of no change in preference with the addition of a third chain."

So the direction and magnitude of the CRR violation is *predicted a priori* by delay reduction, which makes this the most theoretically pointed deletion study in the operant literature — CRR is not merely rejected, it is out-predicted by a rival model on the same data.

## Usability verdict

**CRR-TEST-BUT-NUMBERS-NOT-PRINTED** — recorded as such on the honest grounds that I could not obtain the article at all, so I cannot certify what is or is not printed in it. The design is, on Fantino's own two independent descriptions, exactly the deletion design the project wants (two-key concurrent chains ↔ three-key concurrent chains over the same alternatives, with explicit CRR tests), and the paper demonstrably works in choice proportions. If it prints per-subject or even aggregate proportions for both menus it would be a first-rate hit and would upgrade to CRR-TEST-WITH-PRINTED-MATRICES.

Recommended next step for whoever has library access: pull JEP:ABP 9(2):132–146 directly and check for a conditions × alternatives table of obtained choice proportions in both the two-key and three-key phases. That single check decides the classification.

Standing caveats regardless: (a) pigeons; (b) concurrent *chains*, so the alternatives are conditioned reinforcers with different terminal-link delays rather than commensurable stimuli — the effective "value" of an alternative depends on the whole schedule context, which is precisely why delay reduction beats CRR here; (c) the CRR violation is a predicted contextual effect (adding a rich third alternative makes the original pair diverge, a poor one makes them converge), which is a *directional* context effect rather than noise, and therefore a much sharper target for a Thurstonian account than an undifferentiated "CRR fails" result.

## Conclusion about CRR quoted verbatim

The authors' own words are **NOT OBTAINED** — see Access above for the ten routes tried. What follows are verbatim second-hand statements of their conclusion, each taken from a full text I did fetch and read.

From Jacob & Fantino (1988), *JEAB* 49, p. 368 — Fantino describing his own prior paper: "Fantino and Dunn (1983) conducted three experiments in which a two-key concurrent-chains schedule was changed to a three-key concurrent-chains schedule. Their results confirmed the above predictions: Preference changed consistently with the delay-reduction hypothesis, even when such consistency required substantial deviations from the constant-ratio rule." (Fetched from `https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1338797?pdf=render`.)

From Fantino & Davison (1983), *JEAB* 40, p. 12: "Fantino and Dunn (1983) assessed the applicability of the delay-reduction hypothesis to choice for three VI schedules. They showed that choice for either two or three outcomes was well described by Equation 1 even when the equation required large violations of the constant-ratio rule (Fantino, 1981; Luce, 1959). The mean absolute deviation of the obtained choice proportions from those required by the three-alternative extension of Equation 1 was .04, averaged over four experiments." (Fetched from `https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1347840?pdf=render`.)

Also relevant, from Jacob & Fantino (1988), p. 368, on the wider pattern: "Some of the results obtained by Fantino and Dunn (1983) were consistent with the constant-ratio rule and some were not."
