# Hodge 1967 — Some further tests of the constant-ratio rule

## Citation

Hodge, M.H. (1967). Some further tests of the constant-ratio rule. *Perception & Psychophysics*, 2(10), 429–437. doi:10.3758/BF03208790. (University of Georgia; supported by Contract AF 19(604)-7299, USAF Electronic Systems Division, Bedford, Massachusetts.)

## Domain and stimuli

Five absolute-identification experiments with **unidimensional** ensembles of 2, 4, or 8 stimulus objects, plus reanalysis/summary of the multidimensional and unidimensional auditory data of Hodge & Pollack (1962).

- **Lifted weights, Experiments 1 and 2.** Eight sand-filled medicine bottles (1¼ × 1¼ × 4⅜ in.) on a turntable, lifted by a blindfolded subject. Experiment 1: 150–206 g in 8 g steps. Experiment 2: 6 g steps. Objects labelled with numerals 1–8 (150 g = "1"); subject responds by speaking a numeral. n = 12 University of Georgia students (8 in Exp. 1, 4 in Exp. 2), daily 1 h sessions over two weeks.
- **Visual area, Experiment 1.** Four series (A–D) of eight projected gray circles varying in area; Stimulus 1 = 50.24 sq. in. in all series; interstimulus area increments 1.55 (A), 3.10 (B), 4.65 (C), 6.20 (D) sq. in., so series ranges stand in 1:2:3:4 ratio and some stimulus pairs recur across series (Table 2 maps this). n = 40 students/high-school seniors, 2 h/day for six consecutive days.
- **Visual brightness, Experiment 2.** n = 12 students, 1 h/day for 11 consecutive days.

## Master and restricted response sets

Yes — this is a textbook master-plus-restricted design, **within subjects**, over the **same physical stimuli**:

- **Master:** the full **8 × 8** identification matrix. All eight objects presented, all eight numeral responses allowed.
- **Restricted:** **4 × 4** and **2 × 2** ensembles. Subsets of the eight objects were presented and only the corresponding subset of numeral responses was in play. Before each new ensemble the subject lifted (or viewed) the objects in that ensemble and was told their identity, so the reduced response set was explicit.

Lifted weights Exp. 1: each subject judged 34 sequences of 96 items — four sequences of 8 objects, two sequences each of **8 different 4-object ensembles**, and one sequence each of **14 different 2-object ensembles**; ensemble-size order randomized. Exp. 2: four sequences of 8 objects plus **6 four-object** and **12 two-object** ensembles. Summed over sequences and subjects, each stimulus in each ensemble was presented 384 times (Exp. 1) or 192 times (Exp. 2). Visual area: 4 × 4 and 2 × 2 matrices collected in all four series (432 presentations per stimulus in Series D, fewer in A–C).

The CRR test is a genuine out-of-sample prediction: proportions in the 4 × 4 and 2 × 2 tests are **predicted by renormalizing the 8 × 8 master matrix** and compared against what was actually observed. From p. 431: "The abscissa is the predicted proportions, derived from the 8 by 8 matrix. The ordinate is the obtained proportions, observed in the 4 by 4 tests. The CRR states that the predicted and obtained proportions should fall exactly on the 45[°] line (Clarke, 1957)." Likewise (p. 431): "The 2 by 2 curve is based on the observed confusability of the pairs while the 8 by 8 and 4 by 4 curves are the confusability scores derived by means of the CRR from appropriate 8 by 8 and 4 by 4 matrices."

Analysis is on **grouped (aggregate) data**: "Grouped data were employed in all calculations of the present experiments" (p. 431).

## What numbers are printed or deposited

**No confusion matrices are printed.** This is the fatal limitation. What is printed:

- **Figure 1** (lifted weights Exp. 1) and **Figure 3** (visual area Series A): scatterplots of obtained (ordinate) against CRR-predicted (abscissa) response proportions for every individual cell of all 4 × 4 and 2 × 2 matrices, with the 45° identity line. The cell-level CRR test is therefore *visible* but only as unlabelled plot symbols — recoverable only by graph digitization, and with no way to identify which cell is which.
- **Figure 2:** confusability score (mean proportion of incorrect responses) as a function of interstimulus spacing, three curves (observed 2 × 2, CRR-derived from 4 × 4, CRR-derived from 8 × 8).
- **Table 1** (p. 429–430, lifted weights Exps. 1 and 2) and **Table 3** (p. 432, all single- and multidimensional experiments, split into 4 × 4 and 2 × 2 blocks): summary statistics only — matrix size, mean proportion correct, mean absolute obtained-minus-predicted difference, percentage of absolute differences exceeding .10 (Clarke's criterion), and mean signed diagonal difference. Also split by first/second half after rank-ordering matrices by proportion correct. Example rows from Table 1, Exp. 1: 8×8 mean prop. correct .389; 4×4 .617, mean |diff| .039, 6.2% > .10, algebraic diagonal diff +.017; 2×2 .868, mean |diff| .084, 21.4% > .10, +.044.
- **Table 2** (p. 432): the stimulus design for the four visual-area series (which numbered stimuli appear in which series), not data.
- **Table 4** (p. 433): "Obtained (in parentheses) and Predicted Confusability" — the only genuinely cell-level printed numbers. For selected stimulus pairs, indexed by ensemble range and interstimulus spacing, it gives the CRR-derived confusability alongside the observed 2 × 2 confusability, e.g. spacing 4 / range 0–4: predicted .023 (obtained .105), .052 (.175), .188 (.064) for pairs 1,5 / 1,3 / 1,2. This is a partial, off-diagonal-only slice — nowhere near a master matrix.

**Deposit — and why it is almost certainly dead.** Footnote 3 (p. 437): "Tables, similar to Table 1 and which summarize the data of all the visual experiments, have been deposited in the office of this Journal. Copies are available free upon request. A portion of this information is presented in Table 3." Note carefully: the deposit is described as *summary tables like Table 1*, not raw matrices. So even if the 1967 Psychonomic Press deposit could be recovered (I found no trace of it), it would not supply the 8 × 8 master matrices.

## Usability verdict

**CRR-TEST-BUT-NUMBERS-NOT-PRINTED.**

The design is exactly right — arguably the single best-designed candidate in this sweep: five within-subject experiments, three sense modalities, an 8 × 8 master matrix with high trial counts, and empirically collected 4 × 4 and 2 × 2 restricted-response-set data over the identical physical stimuli, with the CRR scored as a genuine out-of-sample renormalization prediction.

But the printed numbers **do not suffice** to score a full-menu-calibrated prediction against CRR. To calibrate a parameter-free Thurstone model on full-menu shares you need the 8 × 8 master matrix, and it is not printed for any of the five experiments — only aggregate discrepancy statistics (Tables 1 and 3), a partial pairwise confusability table (Table 4), and scatterplots (Figures 1 and 3). Digitizing Figure 1 or 3 would recover a cloud of (predicted, obtained) pairs with no cell identity and no master matrix, which cannot support a Thurstone fit.

Two salvage routes, both partial:
1. **Hodge & Pollack (1962)**, *J. Exp. Psychol.* 63, 129–142, is the auditory companion whose data are re-summarized in Table 3 here. That paper is the place to look for printed auditory matrices; it is not in this candidate set but is the obvious next fetch.
2. **The 1961 technical reports** (Hodge, Crawford & Piercy, "The constant-ratio rule and visual displays"; Hodge, Piercy & Crawford, "The constant-ratio rule and lifted weights") are near-certainly the earlier, longer reports of these same visual and lifted-weights experiments — the 1967 acknowledgments thank "Morris J. Crawford, Mary Piercy" for data collection and cite the same AF contract. Tech reports of that era routinely printed full appendix matrices. See the notes on candidate 9 in the sweep summary; DTIC AD0264314 was unreachable during this sweep.

## Conclusion about CRR quoted verbatim

"In conclusion, the present experiments demonstrate that: (a) CRR predictions are somewhat better for multidimensional than for unidimensional stimuli because of the absence of large differential amounts of stimulus and response confusion in multidimensional situations; (b) the CRR is sensitive to the effects of practice and stimulus spacing-range variables; (c) there is little difference in CRR predictions for auditory, visual, and kinesthetic stimulus dimensions; and (d) the operation of response biases make testing of the CRR with single dimensional stimuli very difficult. More satisfactory tests of the CRR will be possible only when response biases can either be controlled experimentally or their influence negated theoretically by adding a bias parameter to the model." (pp. 436–437)

From the abstract (p. 429): "The results demonstrated: (a) The CRR predicts equally well the response proportions of single dimensional visual, kinesthetic, and auditory stimulus ensembles, but less well than those for multidimensional auditory stimuli. (b) Better predictions are obtained with four than with two stimulus objects. (c) The CRR is sensitive to variations in the spacing and range of the stimulus ensembles and to practice on the task. It is concluded that the rule tends to fail whenever stimulus conditions elicit differential amounts of stimulus and response confusion."

On the 2 × 2 (most restricted) menus specifically, p. 431: "The less accurate prediction of the 2 by 2 proportions is reflected by the relatively large values of the difference measures for these matrices. Of particular interest is the finding that the grand mean of the systematic differences is approximately two and one-half times larger than the mean of the 4 by 4 matrices (+.044 vs +.017)."
