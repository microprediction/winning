# Lee 1968 — Detection theory, micromatching, and the constant-ratio rule

## Citation

Lee, W. (1968). Detection theory, micromatching, and the constant-ratio rule.
*Perception & Psychophysics*, 4(4), 217–219.
doi:10.3758/BF03206305

Full text obtained and read. **This is the most important single item in the forward sweep,
and it is not a dataset.** It is the parameter-free Gaussian prediction of the constant-ratio
rule's failure, computed by numerical integration in 1968, tabulated, and never once carried
out on real confusion matrices. It is filed here rather than only in `PRIOR_ART.md` so that
the next pass over this literature cannot miss it.

## Domain and stimuli

None — there are no subjects, no stimuli and no data. Lee examines "a number of hypothetical
stimulus configurations" of three normal distributions, in three geometries (see Fig. 1):

- **univariate**: three normals on a continuum, Distributions 1–2 equidistant from 2–3;
- **bivariate symmetric**: means at the vertices of an equilateral triangle;
- **bivariate orthogonal**: means at the vertices of an isosceles right triangle.

The Gaussian assumptions are exactly Case V, stated at p. 217: "whenever a stimulus is
presented to an S it gives rise internally to a random independent sample point from a normal
(Gaussian) univariate or bivariate probability distribution… The standard deviations for all
distributions in a set or subset were taken to be equal across stimuli and across dimensions.
For bivariate stimuli, the covariance of the distributions was taken to be zero."

Three competing accounts are compared:

1. **Detection theory (DT)** — deterministic cutoffs/boundaries partitioning the sample space,
   placed to maximize P(correct) under equal priors and a symmetric payoff.
2. **Micromatching (MM)** — Lee's own probabilistic rule, integrating
   r_j = ∫∫ f_j / (f_1 + f_2 + f_3) dx dy, i.e. respond with the posterior probability that the
   sample came from stimulus j.
3. **The CRR / Luce's axiom** — the ratio is invariant, so the "constancy" equals 1.0.

**Important discrimination for this project.** Lee's Gaussian arm is a *decision-bound*
model — one sample point in a shared space, carved by optimal cutoffs — not an
independent-race Thurstone model where each alternative draws its own latent and the argmax
wins. For the univariate three-stimulus case these are genuinely different geometries and
give different numbers. Micromatching is a posterior/ratio rule, also not a race. So Lee is
the closest ancestor of the project's *quantity* but does not compute the project's *map*.

## Master and restricted response sets

Structurally exactly right, and entirely hypothetical: "All the cases considered concern sets
of three stimuli and subsets of two of these stimuli" (p. 217). The full menu is N = 3, the
restricted menu is N = 2, and the conditioning stimulus is required to survive the
restriction — "The signal on which the response probabilities are conditional is the same for
both sets, and therefore, must be present in both the two- and three-signal sets" (p. 218).

Under restriction the decision rule is recomputed from scratch rather than renormalized: for
the two-stimulus subset "a single cutoff point should be set down midway between the means of
the two distributions" (p. 217). That is precisely the project's move — the Gaussian model is
re-solved on the surviving menu with no free parameters, and the result is compared against
what renormalization would have said.

Lee's statistic is the project's statistic. With r′ denoting the three-signal probabilities
and r the two-signal probabilities, he defines the **constancy**

    c_ij = (r_i / r_j) / (r′_i / r′_j)

and notes (p. 218): "According to the CRR, c_ij = 1.0; however, as we shall see, there is no
necessity that c_ij = 1.0 for detection theory or micromatching."

## What numbers are printed or deposited

**Table 1 (p. 218), printed in full**, giving constancies for DT and MM at d′ = 0.0, 0.5, 1.0,
1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 for six configurations — univariate cases (1) r₁/r₂:s₁, (2) r₁/r₃:s₁,
(3) r₁/r₂:s₂; bivariate symmetric (4); bivariate orthogonal (5) and (6). d′ is "always the
distance between the means for the subset of two stimuli."

The departures from renormalization are large and, in the univariate cases, monotone in d′ in
opposite directions depending on which ratio is taken:

| d′ | uni (1) DT | uni (1) MM | uni (2) DT | uni (2) MM | uni (3) DT | uni (3) MM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| 0.5 | 0.44 | 0.92 | 0.96 | 0.99 | 0.33 | 0.90 |
| 1.0 | 0.78 | 0.86 | 0.85 | 0.97 | 0.56 | 0.78 |
| 1.5 | 0.94 | 0.91 | 0.68 | 0.93 | 0.71 | 0.73 |
| 2.0 | 0.99 | 0.96 | 0.51 | 0.84 | 0.81 | 0.77 |
| 2.5 | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0.34 | 0.74 | 0.89 | 0.84 |
| 3.0 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.22 | 0.62 | 0.93 | 0.91 |

Bivariate constancies stay far closer to 1.0 (DT symmetric 0.90–1.00; orthogonal 0.67–0.94).

Two analytic identities are also given (p. 219): c₁₂ = r′₁/(r′₁ + r′₂) for univariate case (1),
and the corresponding form for case (3) — closed-form, parameter-free, and directly checkable.
Note c_ji = 1/c_ij, so the sign of the departure is a matter of which way the ratio is taken;
Lee standardizes on c ≤ 1.0.

Nothing is deposited and nothing is empirical.

## Usability verdict

**NOT-A-RESTRICTED-SET-STUDY** as a dataset — there is no data of any kind, so nothing can be
calibrated on it or scored against it.

**But it is the single most important citation for the project**, for four reasons:

1. **It is the same quantity.** Lee's constancy c is exactly the ratio the project measures:
   observed restricted-menu odds divided by the odds renormalization predicts. CRR says 1.0.
2. **It is parameter-free and Gaussian.** Equal-variance independent normals, zero covariance,
   decision rule re-solved on the surviving menu, nothing fitted. The only difference from
   the project's map is decision-bound versus independent race — a real difference that must
   be stated, not glossed.
3. **It predicts where to look.** The departures concentrate in the **univariate**
   configurations, and Lee uses this to explain Hodge & Pollack's empirical generalization
   that CRR holds better for multidimensional than unidimensional stimuli. That is a directional,
   pre-registered-in-1968 prediction about *which* stimulus geometries should break
   renormalization — and it matches Townsend & Landon's later finding that the subset which
   deleted a survivor's near-substitute was the one CRR could not predict.
4. **It asks for exactly the data this project is assembling, and was ignored.** See the
   verbatim request below. The sweep's central historical finding is that the field did not
   comply: percent-correct summaries and scatterplots dominate, which is why only Townsend &
   Landon (1982) and the Wills CAM1 deposit turned out to be scoreable.

What the project adds over Lee: an independent-race Thurstone map rather than optimal cutoffs;
calibration by inverting *observed* full-menu shares rather than positing d′ on hypothetical
configurations; and an actual out-of-sample score on real matrices. What the project cannot
claim: that the Gaussian departure from renormalization is a new idea, or that its size and
direction were unknown. Both were tabulated in 1968.

## Conclusion about CRR quoted verbatim

From the abstract (p. 217):

> "The conformance of detection theory and micromatching with the constant-ratio rule (CRR)
> was determined by calculations for sets of three stimuli and subsets of two stimuli. For an
> arbitrary selection of sets, the CRR would be expected to hold quite well if either
> detection theory or micromatching lay behind the responses. For specific stimulus
> configurations, however, detection theory and micromatching differ considerably from one
> another and from the CRR. These configurations are found particularly for the univariate
> distributions, and this corresponds to empirical findings that the CRR holds better for
> multidimensional than for unidimensional stimuli. It is shown how the differences between
> the three theories in the implied constancies of ratios can be used in diagnosis of the
> basis of empirical confusion matrices."

On the decisive point that renormalization is not implied by a Gaussian model (p. 218):

> "According to the CRR, c_ij = 1.0; however, as we shall see, there is no necessity that
> c_ij = 1.0 for detection theory or micromatching."

On the falsifying power of the numbers (p. 218):

> "Note that most of the constancies are close to 1.0, and for many situations the CRR would
> hold to a good approximation even if the postulates of detection theory or of micromatching
> lay behind the responses. On the other hand, for some situations, the constancy for
> detection theory, micromatching, or both differs considerably from 1.0, in which case real
> data with some reasonable precision to it must lead to rejection of at least one of the
> three formulations, and possibly of two."

On where a critical test must be run (p. 219):

> "It seems clear from the table that if one were selecting cases arbitrarily, the CRR could
> be expected to hold quite well whether detection theory or micromatching were in actuality
> behind the responses. If one wished for a critical test, however, one would not choose cases
> arbitrarily, but would choose cases where the prediction of the CRR, detection theory, and
> micromatching differ considerably. These would be univariate cases, since here is where the
> notable differences occur, as far as can be told from the results at hand."

On why the CRR looked successful, and Clarke's own later doubts (p. 219):

> "The results in Table I correspond to, and may help explain a generalization noted by Hodge
> and Pollack (1962) and Hodge (1967): the CRR does better for the multidimensional stimuli
> than for the unidimensional stimuli. There is nothing in Luce's choice theory to indicate
> such a result, nor did Clarke indicate any such expectation in his original presentation of
> CRR. Clarke later put forward doubts on the adequacy of the CRR for unidimensional stimuli,
> but these doubts were based on empirical work."

(That last sentence independently corroborates the unpublished Clarke 1959 negative documented
in `clarke1957.md`.)

**And the request the field ignored for fifty-eight years** (p. 219) — the best single epigraph
available for this project:

> "In view of the implied effects of the individual stimulus configurations on constancy, I
> would suggest that investigators of the CRR give more detail on individual response
> probabilities for specific configurations, rather than simply giving gross plots and gross
> statistics. If this level of detail is not possible for a journal article, at least the
> availability of the data could be advertized."
