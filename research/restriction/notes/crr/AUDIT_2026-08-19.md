# Numerical audit of the manuscript, and two new sources

Audit run against HEAD at 10:31 (`502854a`, 1,592 lines); the paper was being edited during
the pass, so line numbers may have shifted. Everything below was checked against the code or
the committed outputs, and the distinction between "error" and "could not verify" is kept.

## Verified clean, and worth knowing it is clean

- **All 39 scoreboard rows.** Every gain and tail, sign included, matches the per-population
  section and the committed output. No row disagrees with its section.
- **Cross-table consistency.** Each population's gain is identical across the scoreboard,
  `tab:menus`, `tab:after`, `tab:more`, `tab:heldout`, `tab:null` and `tab:getty`, and
  `excess = gain - null median` holds exactly except the rounding cases below.
- **Respondent counts exact.** Re-running the loaders gives ranking n summing to 147,346;
  Costa-Gomes has 373 subjects (234 + 139) of which 364 enter, forced-choice 76 and 61;
  147,346 + 373 = 147,719.
- **Townsend & Landon, Wills, Getty, Stewart tones re-ran and reproduce**, including the
  s1/A/{A,E,X} triple quoted in the text and the 0-versus-510 withdrawn-response count.
- The **negligible-mass example**, the **by-rank decomposition**, "monotone in rank",
  "crossing between fourth and fifth", "contributions sum to +0.0043", the lottery gradient
  (0.3567 to 0.2573, ln 6 = 1.7918), and "race lower in 12 of 12" all check out.
- The pooled forced-choice gain of +0.0265 that the repo's own `demo/check_tables.py` cannot
  trace **is correct**: 137 subjects, 3,425 choices, +0.0265, null -0.0043, excess +0.0308,
  p = 0.010. It needs a committed output file, not a correction.

## Errors to fix, most serious first

1. **The heterogeneity table omits its one awkward row.** `heterogeneity_limit.py` produces
   eight rows; the printed table has seven. The omitted sigma = 6 (top share 0.372, observed
   lambda 0.2248, Case V 0.2302, ratio 0.98) is the single row where the ratio is
   non-monotone in sigma, and the caption says the table is "reproduced by
   `python heterogeneity_limit.py`". Every printed cell does match the output and the stated
   parameters are all correct, so this is a selective omission rather than a wrong number --
   which is why it is first. Print the row.
2. **The headline row's tail is its own floor, and the caption says otherwise.** The caption
   says tails are "floored at 1/201 by two hundred replicates". News slates `.016` comes from
   **60** replicates, so 1/61 = 0.0164: that tail is the floor, not a measured 1.6 per cent.
   Wills `.002` comes from **400** replicates, floor 1/401. Either raise the replicate count
   on the news row or state its floor.
3. **Undisclosed truncation.** The text says 6,135 nested slate pairs; the table says "6,000
   pairs". `mind_slates.py` does `prepare(pairs[:6000], slates)`, so 135 pairs are dropped and
   the paper never says so.
4. **The 3.4 factor cannot be reproduced.** "misprices pairs and triples by up to a factor of
   3.4" appears in two places. No script in `research/restriction` produces it;
   `exotics_human.txt` tops out at 1.53 on ratio percentiles. The figure exists only as prose
   in `STATUS.md`. Either regenerate it or drop it.
5. **Abstract count is wrong.** "twenty-four observing the smaller menu and twelve inducing
   subset choice from rankings" sums to 36, not 39. The scoreboard's Restriction column is
   observed menus 24, ranking-induced 12, observed impressions 1, observed ballots 1, observed
   removals 1. Slates, ballots and Wikipedia all observe the smaller menu, so the split is
   **27 / 12**. (39 and 30 are both right; the earlier "thirty-four" was stale and is gone.)
6. **"The four losses in Table 1 are one task."** There are nine loss rows, and the scoreboard
   section already says "eight of the nine".
7. **"a census of sixty-seven sources, one note each."** There are 126 note files, about 98
   distinct sources after normalising author-year duplicates, and 19 sources have between two
   and five notes, so "one note each" is false as well as the count.
8. **"None of the eleven entries is a preference task"** above a census table with nine rows.
9. **"The pairwise gain is two to four times the all-subsets aggregate."** True for 5 of 12
   collections. It fails for Puzzles 1.9, Dots 1.7, Netflix 1.4, GSS job values 1.2, Sports
   1.2, Political goals 1.14, and Occupational prestige **0.73**, where the pairwise gain is
   *smaller*. Three of the seven rows printed in that very table break the claim.
10. **"Occupational prestige is the one non-monotone row."** Sports participation is also
    non-monotone (+.0058, +.0062, +.0051, +.0036, +.0019, +.0001) and also omitted. Relatedly
    "the last entry in each row is exactly zero" is contradicted by the table's own printed
    `-.0000` entries: the coincidence is exact in theory but ~1e-4 in a pipeline whose
    calibration residual runs to ~1e-3.
11. **"The six-symbol gain is the largest here outside news slates."** +0.0278 is sixth in the
    scoreboard, behind both Getty conditions, Consumer goods 1 forced, Occupational prestige
    and Wills. True only if "here" means that one table.
12. **"a SOURCE.md per collection."** Nine exist. None for Costa-Gomes, Wills, GSS, Jester,
    Sushi, PrefLib, MIND, clickstream or the ballots.
13. **"The one entry needing only transcription is the oldest, published in 1960."** Clarke
    1957 sits two rows above it, and see the Pollack and Decker finding below, which removes
    the 1960 entry from the transcribable category altogether.
14. **Sushi excess mixes smoothing conventions.** +0.0111 - (-0.0062) = +0.0173, printed
    +0.0174. The alpha = 0.5 run gives null median -0.0063 and excess +0.0175; -0.0062 is the
    alpha = 0 value. The repo's own `check_tables.py` flags this cell too.
15. **"five to thirteen per cent"** should be 4.9 to 12.4; the 13.3 came from recomputing off
    rounded printed values. **"the excess exceeds the raw gain in all twelve remaining rows"**
    is 13 rows (12 is right for collections). **Lottery units 4,099** is total usable
    observations where every other row in that column reports scored units, which is 3,928.
16. **Rouder as "the largest and best-powered disagreement in the paper."** Larger absolute
    gains exist, and the recognition interval is called "the tightest in the paper" at width
    .0018 against Rouder's .0072. Defensible only as the largest disagreement running
    *against* the race.
17. **"every row of the sound-identification and letter matrices reproduces its printed
    total"** -- two Townsend and Landon rows are dropped for unresolvable errors in the
    published table, which the paper does not say.
18. Could not verify, because the inputs are external archives rather than repo extracts:
    the 153,727 impression count, and the tightened-filter page counts 3,544 / 817 / 121
    (only the 3,544 row is in `clickstream.txt`).
19. `lambda_table.py` has **no committed output**, and the GSS "truth between the two
    defaults" claim rests on it. Worth knowing that in 9 of 12 collections the observed lambda
    *exceeds* the race's, so GSS is the exception rather than the pattern.

## Duffy and Smith (2025): a confirmation, not a threat

*An economist and a psychologist form a line: what can imperfect perception of length tell us
about stochastic choice?* Theory and Decision 99(3):701-734, CC BY. Data at OSF `f7gu4`,
mirrored to `research/restriction/data/duffy_smith/`.

Their abstract reports errors "better described as having a Gumbel distribution rather than a
normal distribution" with "evidence consistent with the independence from irrelevant
alternatives (IIA) property", which reads as a direct contradiction. It is not. **Their stimuli
are line lengths**: "objects are valued according to only a single attribute with a continuous
measure". That is exactly the unidimensional continuum on which the tone matrices, the
twelve-line identification data and one Getty condition already have renormalization winning.
The paper predicts their result.

Two things make it the strongest independent check available. Value is **induced with real
money**, so utility is known rather than inferred, which is cleaner than any confusion matrix.
And they reach a compatible conclusion by an entirely different paradigm while citing the same
literature: Yellott 1977 by name, "Case V of Thurstone (1927a)", Luce, and the CRR chain
including Clarke 1957 and Morgan 1974.

Scored two ways, and the answer is construction-dependent in magnitude but not in direction.

| construction | calibrate → predict | advantage to renormalization | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| fitted, one scale parameter each, actual trial lengths | n=2 → n=3..6 | +0.0059 | 3.93 |
| " | **n=6 → n=2** | **+0.0037** | **2.28** |
| " | n=5,6 → n=2,3 | +0.0051 | 4.54 |
| parameter-free, alternatives identified by length rank | n=6 → n=2..5 | +0.0009, CI [-0.0008, +0.0019] | -- |

The fitted comparison is the right one for this dataset and is decisive; the parameter-free
version is a draw. The reason is a genuine limitation rather than a discrepancy: **lengths are
redrawn every trial**, so there is no fixed menu carrying stable shares to invert, and
identifying alternatives by rank pools over heterogeneous configurations. Report the fitted
numbers and say plainly that this is a fitted comparison, not the paper's protocol.

Two liabilities to meet head on rather than let a referee raise:

- They claim the **opposite generality**: "IIA could be a general feature of choice, and
  violations of IIA only occur in specific choice settings" (p. 728). Every
  restriction-failure claim in the paper must therefore carry its domain on its face.
- Their **IIA test has little power**. On 500 replications over their own 8,628 configurations
  it rejects 5.6 to 8.4 per cent of the time when the truth is Case V at their own fitted
  sigma, against 4.8 to 6.4 per cent under Luce. Case V predicts a top-two drift of only
  +0.009 to +0.013 from n=2 to n=6 against a 0.033 standard error in the sparsest cell. They
  simulated power for the AIC comparison, where they had it, and not for the IIA test.
- It is a **four-paper programme**: Duffy and Smith 2025a (*J. Econ. Psych.* 106:102787, 56
  triples, Product Rule, directly on restriction), Duffy, Gussman and Smith 2021, and
  Branas-Garza and Smith 2024. A referee who knows one knows all four.

## Pollack and Decker (1960): the census entry is wrong

Acquired in full from Internet Archive microfilm serials, item
`sim_language-and-speech_january-march-1960_3_1`, unrestricted; the article is journal pages
1-6. Every other route failed and is recorded in
`digitized/pollack_decker1960_SOURCE.md`. It is also AFCRC Technical Note 59-17, so a DTIC copy
plausibly exists; Pollack was at AFCRC Bedford, not Michigan, so that earlier lead was wrong.

**The three overlapping 4x4 subset matrices were never tabulated.** They were run, then reduced
to mean absolute deviations, one scalar per set per signal-to-noise level, before publication.
So this cannot supply master-and-subset pairs to score two restriction maps, and the census
entry "printed; digitize" is wrong. Correct it to: matrices reduced to summary deviations, not
scoreable.

What was recovered is still worth having: four observed **8x8 masters** over one fixed
eight-alternative menu across a 12 dB sweep, mean diagonal 78.6, 68.4, 48.1, 28.9 per cent.
256 of 256 cells digitized with no NA. The integer-recovery trick does **not** work here -- at
n = 360 per row the granularity is 0.28 per cent against 1 per cent printing, so counts are not
identified, unlike Townsend and Landon. Verification instead came from row sums (32/32 within
±1), count feasibility (32/32), a textual anchor, and the strong check: Figure 2 draws one
labelled arrow per off-diagonal above a stated cutoff, and arrow membership and value agree
with the digitized matrices in all four panels, 48/48 exact with zero set differences. That
bites precisely where the microfilm OCR fails.

Table 1's deviations are digitized separately and make a validation target for any CRR
implementation.
