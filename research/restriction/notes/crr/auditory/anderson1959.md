# Anderson (1959), AFCRC-TN-58-60 — CRR with visually presented monosyllables, from the speech laboratory

## Citation

Anderson, C. D. (1959). *The constant-ratio rule as a predictor of confusions among various stimuli of

Indiana University.

Same C. D. Anderson as the second author of Clarke & Anderson (1957). AFCRC = Air Force Cambridge

## Stimuli and master response set

**Visually presented monosyllables at brief exposure duration** — so strictly this is a *visual*
experiment, but it is produced by the speech laboratory, on speech material (monosyllables), as a direct
extension of Clarke's auditory work, and both Hodge and Townsend & Landon classify it inside the same
literature. Filed here for that reason, with the modality flagged.

Hodge (1967, p. 429), verbatim, listing it among CRR successes:

> "A number of tests has shown that the CRR makes satisfactory predictions of the confusion patterns
> associated with multidimensional objects such as various speech stimuli (Clarke, 1957; Clarke &
> Anderson, 1957; Carterette & Wyman, 1961; Pollack & Decker, 1960), words (Egan, 1957), visually
> presented monosyllables (Anderson, 1959), and multidimensional auditory tones (Hodge & Pollack, 1962)."

Townsend & Landon (1982, p. 121) note its significance for the visual branch, verbatim:

> "It has been employed or tested in other contexts (e.g., auditory recognition; Clarke, 1957) but not,
> to our knowledge, in visual letter recognition (Anderson, Note 3, employed visually presented
> monosyllables)."

Master set size, monosyllable list, and exposure durations: unknown.

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**NEEDS LIBRARY ACCESS (or DTIC retrieval) — currently unlocatable. Low priority, for two reasons.**

First, the modality is wrong for this directory: visually presented monosyllables belong to the visual
branch, where Townsend & Landon (1982) is the definitive and *obtainable* study (its PDF is already in
hand; it prints its own alphabetic confusion data and tests the CRR against four interpretations of the
similarity choice model with proper statistics). Anything Anderson could show about visual CRR, Townsend
& Landon show better and with a significance test.

Second, the acquisition cost is high and uncertain, whereas Egan's Technical Report 57-50 — the other
grey-literature target — sits on genuinely auditory speech data and should be chased first.

Revisit only if (a) exposure duration turns out to have been manipulated in a way that gives a clean
discriminability axis, and (b) the journal literature has already been exhausted.

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

**No text from this document was obtained, so there is no verbatim quotation from Anderson.**

The only sourced statements are second-hand, and they agree. Hodge (1967, p. 429) places it among the
studies showing "*that the CRR makes satisfactory predictions*" for, among other materials, "*visually
presented monosyllables (Anderson, 1959)*".

Townsend & Landon (1982, p. 122), verbatim:

> "Several tests of the CRR have been conducted, and most have concluded that the CRR does make
> satisfactory predictions with choice data (Anderson, Note 3; Clarke, 1957, 1959; Clarke & Anderson,
> 1957; Egan, 1957, Note 4; Hodge, 1967; Hodge & Pollack, 1962; Pollack & Decker, 1960). However, there is
> some question as to the validity of the results of these past studies due to what was at the time a lack
> of any statistical test of the deviations of the results from the predictions of the CRR."

So Anderson concluded FOR the rule, on the same untested basis as everyone else in the 1957-1962 group.
Townsend & Landon's own verdict on the visual case, reached with statistics, went the other way — the
CRR and the strictest interpretation of the similarity choice model "*provided a reasonable first
approximation to the data. However, they proved inferior to the least strict SCM*" (Townsend & Landon
1982, abstract, verbatim).
