# Clarke (1957), Experiment II — monosyllables (words), master + subsets

## Citation

Clarke, F. R. (1957). Constant-Ratio Rule for Confusion Matrices in Speech Communication.
*Journal of the Acoustical Society of America*, **29**(6), 715-720. doi:10.1121/1.1909023
Hearing and Communication Laboratory, Department of Psychology, Indiana University.

Second of the three experiments in the founding CRR paper. Experiment I (CV syllables) and
Experiment III (digits) have their own notes in this directory.

## Stimuli and master response set

Spoken **monosyllabic words** in noise, closed-set identification. Clarke's abstract says only:
"*Two more experiments using monosyllables and digits were then conducted to test further the
rule.*" Almost certainly PB (phonetically balanced) monosyllable lists, given the Indiana Hearing
and Communication Laboratory's standard materials of the period and the fact that the same JASA
volume 29 carries Clarke's "Remarks on Rare PB Words" (JASA 29, 751, doi:10.1121/1.1909046) — but
the word list and master set size are NOT recoverable without the article.

Lee (1968, *Percept. Psychophys.* 4, 217-219, p. 217) confirms the three stimulus classes:
"*Clarke (1957) published results utilizing spoken syllables, words, and numbers, with highly
favorable results.*"

## Restricted response sets (state whether nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested** — subsets of the monosyllable master set, with stimulus set and allowable response set
restricted together, per Clarke's stated design assumption that "*the only variables which differ
systematically in obtaining the two matrices are the different sets of messages and the allowable
responses*".

Subset sizes and count are NOT recoverable from the abstract. For calibration, the companion paper
Clarke & Anderson (1957) used a 10-item master with two 5-item subsets on words; Experiment II here
is the practised-subject precursor to that design.

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

Clarke concluded FOR the rule across all three experiments; the abstract does not separate the
verdict by experiment. Verbatim (author's abstract):

> "Three experiments are reported which give support to an empirical rule which may be used for
> predicting the entries in a closed confusion matrix for any subset of items drawn from a master
> set of items with a known confusion matrix. ... Two more experiments using monosyllables and
> digits were then conducted to test further the rule."

Later verdict on the same data, verbatim from Townsend & Landon (1982, *JMP* 25, p. 122):

> "Morgan (1974) applied his likelihood ratio test to the data reported by Clarke (1957) and Egan
> (1957) and found their data to depart significantly from the predictions of the CRR."
