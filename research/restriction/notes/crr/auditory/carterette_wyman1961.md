# Carterette & Wyman (1961) — "Confusion matrices in speech communication", conference paper, apparently never published

## Citation

Carterette, E. C., & Wyman, M. J. (1961). *Confusion matrices in speech communication.* Paper read at
the Psychonomic Society, New York, August 1961.

Verbatim transcription of the citation from the reference list of Hodge (1967), *Perception &
Psychophysics* 2, p. 437 (PDF in hand):

> "Carterette, E. C., & Wyman. M. J. Confusion matrices in speech communication. Paper read at
> Psychonomic Society. New York, August, 1961."

Edward C. Carterette was at UCLA (Department of Psychology / Institute for Human Learning), and later
co-edited the *Handbook of Perception*. This appears to be the only trace of the work.

## Stimuli and master response set

**Unknown.** The title says "confusion matrices in speech communication", and Hodge (1967, p. 429) groups
it with the studies using "*multidimensional objects such as various speech stimuli*", verbatim:

> "A number of tests has shown that the CRR makes satisfactory predictions of the confusion patterns
> associated with multidimensional objects such as various speech stimuli (Clarke, 1957; Clarke &
> Anderson, 1957; Carterette & Wyman, 1961; Pollack & Decker, 1960)"

So: speech material, multidimensional (i.e. phonemes/syllables/words rather than tones on a single
continuum), master matrix plus at least one restriction — otherwise it would not appear in a list of CRR
tests. Nothing more can be said.

## Restricted response sets (state whether nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Presumed nested**, on the sole basis that Hodge lists it as a test of the CRR, which in this literature
means master-to-subset prediction. Not verified.

## What numbers are printed (master matrix? restricted matrices? counts or proportions? which table numbers?)

**Nothing is printed anywhere that I can locate.** A paper "read at" a society meeting in 1961, with no
subsequent journal publication traced, means there is very likely no published matrix at all — at most a
one-paragraph meeting abstract in the Psychonomic Society programme, and possibly not even that (the
Psychonomic Society was founded in 1959 and its early meeting programmes are not well digitised).

## Usability verdict (usable now / needs digitizing / needs library access / unusable, and why)

**UNUSABLE — treat as a dead end and do not spend further effort.**

The reasoning: a 1961 conference talk with no traceable publication, no DOI, no citing literature beyond
a single reference-list mention in Hodge (1967), and no digital footprint at all. Even if the meeting
programme abstract were found, it would be a paragraph, not a matrix — the same situation as Clarke
(1959), which *is* findable and *is* useless as data. The expected yield does not justify archival work.

Record it in the paper's literature review for completeness — it is a genuine spine member in the sense
that contemporaries counted it as CRR evidence — and mark it as unrecoverable grey literature. Its
existence is itself a small point worth making: part of the reason this branch was never converted into
a benchmark is that some of its evidence never existed in citable, tabulated form in the first place.

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

**No text by Carterette and Wyman was obtained. No verbatim quotation is available.**

The only sourced characterisation of their result is Hodge's, and it is a bare classification rather than
a description. Hodge (1967, *Percept. Psychophys.* 2, p. 429), verbatim:

> "A number of tests has shown that the CRR makes satisfactory predictions of the confusion patterns
> associated with multidimensional objects such as various speech stimuli (Clarke, 1957; Clarke &
> Anderson, 1957; Carterette & Wyman, 1961; Pollack & Decker, 1960), words (Egan, 1957), visually
> presented monosyllables (Anderson, 1959), and multidimensional auditory tones (Hodge & Pollack, 1962)."

Note that Townsend & Landon (1982), whose review is otherwise the most complete, **omit Carterette &
Wyman entirely** from their list of CRR tests (Clarke 1957/1959, Clarke & Anderson 1957, Egan 1957 and
Note 4, Anderson Note 3, Hodge 1967, Hodge & Pollack 1962, Pollack & Decker 1960). By 1982 the work had
already dropped out of the record.
