# Hodge (1967) — five experiments, 8x8 masters with 4x4 and 2x2 subsets; OBTAINED but prints NO matrices

## Citation

Hodge, M. H. (1967). Some further tests of the constant-ratio rule. *Perception & Psychophysics*,
**2**(10), 429-437. doi:10.3758/BF03208790
University of Georgia. Accepted 24 May 1967. Supported by Contract No. AF 19(604)-7299 between the
USAF Electronic Systems Division, Bedford, Massachusetts and the University of Georgia. Criticism
acknowledged from Irwin Pollack and Earl A. Alluisi.

**PDF obtained and fully text-extracted (bronze OA at Springer).**

## Stimuli and master response set

**Not auditory** — that is the first thing to say. Five experiments with **lifted weights** (kinesthetic)
and **visual** stimuli:

- Lifted Weights Experiment 1: eight sand-filled medicine bottles, 150-206 g in **8 g** steps.
- Lifted Weights Experiment 2: same, **6 g** steps.
- Visual Experiment 1 (visual area): four series (A, B, C, D) of eight gray circles, area increments
  1.55 / 3.10 / 4.65 / 6.20 sq. in., stimulus 1 always 50.24 sq. in., so series ranges stand in ratio
  1:2:3:4.
- Visual Experiment 2 (visual brightness): eight brightness levels.

**Master response set is 8 in every experiment (8x8 masters).** Presentations: each stimulus shown 384
times (weights Exp 1), 192 (weights Exp 2), 528/480/480/432 (visual area series A/B/C/D).

The file is filed in this auditory directory for one reason: **Hodge (1967) Table 3 reprints the CRR
summary statistics for all nine auditory conditions of Hodge & Pollack (1962)**, which is otherwise
behind the APA paywall. Those rows are transcribed in `hodge1962.md`. This paper is the free window onto
the auditory data.

## Restricted response sets (state whether nested, overlapping, or a relabelling)

**Nested, at two depths, with overlapping subset families — and this paper states the family sizes
explicitly, which no other paper in the branch does.**

Verbatim (p. 432, visual area experiment): "*The 32 sequences consisted of four sequences of 8 objects,
two sequences each of 7 ensembles of 4 objects, and one sequence each of 14 ensembles of 2 objects.*"

Verbatim (p. 430, lifted weights Exp 1): "*each S was given 34 sequences of 96 items with stimulus
ensembles consisting of 8, 4, or 2 weights. Each S judged four sequences of 8 objects, two sequences each
of 8 ensembles of 4 objects, and one sequence each of 14 ensembles of 2 objects.*"

So: **one 8x8 master; 7 or 8 different 4-subsets; 14 different 2-subsets** — out of 70 possible
quadruples and 28 possible pairs. Heavily overlapping, and deliberately chosen to span a range of
interstimulus spacings. Stimulus set and response set restricted together; subjects were re-shown the
members of each new ensemble and told their identities before each block.

There is also a **relabelling manipulation**, unique in the branch and directly relevant to context
effects. Because the four visual series overlap in physical stimuli, the *same physical pair* appears
under different numeric labels in different series. Verbatim (p. 433): "*To be noted first is the
influence of the 'label' assigned in the 2 by 2 tests upon the observed confusability of a given pair
(compare the entries within parentheses across a given line in Table 4). It is apparent that the observed
confusabilities of certain pairs (e.g., those with a relative spacing of 0-6, 1-4, and 0-3) tend to depend
upon the labels assigned to the circles. However, since other pairs (e.g., 0-12, 0-8, and 0-2) fail to
show this dependency, a systematic relationship between labels and observed confusability is doubtful.*"

## What numbers are printed (master matrix? restricted matrices? counts or proportions? which table numbers?)

**NO CONFUSION MATRICES ARE PRINTED. Not one, master or subset.** This is the key finding about this
paper and it is a clean example of the failure mode the brief anticipates.

What is printed instead:

- **Table 1** — lifted weights, summary only: matrix size (8x8 / 4x4 / 2x2, each split into rank-ordered
  first and second halves), mean proportion correct, mean absolute obtained-minus-predicted difference,
  % of absolute differences exceeding .10, and mean algebraic diagonal difference.
- **Table 2** — schematic of stimulus spacing across visual series A-D (physical layout, not data).
- **Table 3** — the same four summary statistics for **all** experiments including the nine auditory
  conditions from Hodge & Pollack (1962), at 4x4 and 2x2 depth. Transcribed in full in `hodge1962.md`.
- **Table 4** — obtained (in parentheses) and CRR-predicted **confusability scores** (i.e. total wrong-
  response proportion) for selected stimulus *pairs*, by absolute and relative spacing, across series
  A-D. This is the closest thing to cell-level data in the paper: single scalars per pair, not matrices.
- **Figs. 1 and 3** — scatterplots of obtained against CRR-predicted response proportions for all 4x4
  and 2x2 cells. **The individual cell values exist only as ink dots in these scatterplots.** In
  principle digitizable; in practice the reproduction quality in the available PDF is poor and the points
  are unlabelled, so stimulus-response identity is unrecoverable. Not worth attempting.
- **Fig. 2** — confusability as a function of interstimulus spacing.

And the tables that *would* have helped are lost. Footnote 3, verbatim (p. 437): "*Tables, similar to
Table 1 and which summarize the data of all the visual experiments, have been deposited in the office of
this Journal. Copies are available free upon request. A portion of this information is presented in Table
3.*" Even those were summaries, not matrices. A 1967 deposit with a journal office is not recoverable.

**One-line verdict as the brief requests: this paper manipulates response-set size systematically over
five experiments, and publishes only summary difference statistics and unlabelled scatterplots, so it is
unusable as a source of confusion matrices.**

## What the authors concluded about CRR, quoted verbatim where possible

Hodge's abstract, verbatim and in full:

> "Five experiments sought to test the constant-ratio rule (CRR) with single dimensional ensembles composed
> of 2, 4, or 8 stimulus objects. Each S attempted to identify stimuli which varied in weight or in visual
> size or brightness. The results demonstrated: (a) The CRR predicts equally well the response proportions
> of single dimensional visual, kinesthetic, and auditory stimulus ensembles, but less well than those for
> multidimensional auditory stimuli. (b) Better predictions are obtained with four than with two stimulus
> objects. (c) The CRR is sensitive to variations in the spacing and range of the stimulus ensembles and to
> practice on the task. It is concluded that the rule tends to fail whenever stimulus conditions elicit
> differential amounts of stimulus and response confusion."

His closing conclusions, verbatim (pp. 436-437):

> "In conclusion, the present experiments demonstrate that: (a) CRR predictions are somewhat better for
> multidimensional than for unidimensional stimuli because of the absence of large differential amounts of
> stimulus and response confusion in multidimensional situations; (b) the CRR is sensitive to the effects of
> practice and stimulus spacing-range variables; (c) there is little difference in CRR predictions for
> auditory, visual, and kinesthetic stimulus dimensions; and (d) the operation of response biases make
> testing of the CRR with single dimensional stimuli very difficult. More satisfactory tests of the CRR will
> be possible only when response biases can either be controlled experimentally or their influence negated
> theoretically by adding a bias parameter to the model."

That last clause is worth dwelling on for the project: Hodge's proposed fix is **to add a bias
parameter** — i.e. to move from Luce's zero-extra-parameter renormalization to the biased choice /
similarity choice model. The alternative he does not consider is to keep zero parameters and change the
latent distribution from Gumbel to Gaussian. Verbatim, same page:

> "A bias parameter has been proposed by Luce (1963) for his choice theory, but a test of its usefulness is
> beyond the scope of the present report."

On the absence of a test — the branch's central methodological failure, verbatim (p. 430):

> "Although a satisfactory statistical test is not available (Clarke, 1957), inspection of the difference
> measures for the 4 by 4 matrices confirms the accuracy of the predictions shown in Fig. 1; all the
> measures are reasonably small."

On the size-dependent failure, verbatim (p. 430):

> "The less accurate prediction of the 2 by 2 proportions is reflected by the relatively large values of the
> difference measures for these matrices. Of particular interest is the finding that the grand mean of the
> systematic differences is approximately two and one-half times larger than the mean of the 4 by 4 matrices
> (+.044 vs +.017). An examination of the individual 2 by 2 matrices showed that the largest systematic
> differences are associated with stimulus pairs consisting of adjacent objects, e.g., stimuli 7, 8; 5,6; 1,
> 2."

On practice, contradicting Clarke & Anderson's (1957) invariance claim, verbatim (p. 433):

> "All the 4 by 4 and 2 by 2 difference measures in each series declined systematically with practice on the
> 8 by 8 matrix, thus indicating that CRR predictions improve with practice on the task."

On range, verbatim (p. 433):

> "The most important point is that the derived confusabilities become larger as the range of the 8 by 8
> matrix increases... Furthermore, the amount of change in derived confusability tends to become larger with
> decreases in the spacing of a pair."

And on Clarke's own retreat, verbatim (p. 429):

> "In an experiment with simple auditory stimuli, e.g., tones varying in frequency or intensity, Clarke
> (1959) noted that the rule tended to fail. In Clarke's opinion, the rule failed because the ordering
> inherent in single dimensional stimuli produces contextual constraints or biases which, by definition, are
> incompatible with the CRR."
