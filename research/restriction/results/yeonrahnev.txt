Losses are mean nats per cell, a cell being one (observer, dominant item, surviving
alternative). "after" is the menu revealed once the stimulus is gone, which is the
restriction test; "before" is the same pairs announced in advance, which is not, and is
the control. The null resamples the calibration row as well as the pair counts, under a
Luce process whose worths are the observed full-menu shares, so it charges the race for
calibration noise; tail 0.005 is the floor at 200 replicates.

The second table is a weighted mean over cells of the favourite's (that is, the dominant
item's) restricted share, and it is where the honest caveat lives: in the two genuine
restriction arms BOTH maps over-predict. The race's contraction moves in the right
direction and covers 21 per cent of renormalization's error at K=4 and 33 per cent at
K=6, but neither parameter-free default is calibrated here. That over-prediction is Yeon
and Rahnev's own result, arrived at with a fitted model.
