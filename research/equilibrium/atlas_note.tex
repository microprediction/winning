\section{The static object}

Consider a one-shot race among $n$ contestants with performances
$X_1,\dots,X_n$, smallest wins, locations $\mu$ and a smooth joint
density; $p_i(\mu)$ is $i$'s win probability. \citet{cotton2026}
(Proposition 1 there, the photo-finish flux) shows that the derivative
of $i$'s win probability with respect to a rival's location is a
boundary flux,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tie}
w_{ij} \;=\; \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial \mu_j}
\;=\; \int \varphi_{ij}(t,t)\,
\Pr\{X_k > t\ \forall k\ne i,j \mid X_i=X_j=t\}\,dt ,
\end{equation}
the probability density that $i$ and $j$ dead-heat \emph{ahead of the
field}, with $\varphi_{ij}$ the joint density of $(X_i,X_j)$. We call
$w_{ij}$ the \emph{tie density}. The tie densities are symmetric and
nonnegative, and because a common shift of every location moves no
probability, the Jacobian $J$ they fill has zero row sums: it is the
graph Laplacian of the ties. The same reference evaluates $p$, the tie
densities and $J$'s action for all $n$ contestants together on a
lattice of $L$ points in $O(nL)$, by sharing one survival field
$\sum_j \log S_j$ across contestants, with $S_j$ the survival function
of $X_j$; that cost is what makes everything below computable at
scale.

\section{The dynamic object}

Let the performances be Brownian,
\[
X_i(t) \;=\; \mu_i + \sigma\,W_i(t),
\qquad W_1,\dots,W_n \ \text{independent},
\]
so that the time-$T$ marginals form exactly the Gaussian race of
\citet{cotton2026} with idiosyncratic variances $D_i = \sigma^2 T$: the
static machinery prices $p_i(T)=\Pr\{X_i(T)=\min_k X_k(T)\}$ and its
Jacobian at every horizon, in $O(nL)$ per horizon.

Now watch the boundary rather than the marginal. Fix a pair $(i,j)$ and
set $Y = X_i - X_j$, a Brownian motion with drift $\mu_i-\mu_j$ and
quadratic variation $\langle Y\rangle_t = 2\sigma^2 t$. The event
``$i$ and $j$ are the leading pair and tied'' is the particle system
touching $\{X_i = X_j \le X_k\ \forall k\}$. Time spent straddling that
set is measured by a local time: define the \emph{leading-pair
collision local time}
\[
\Lambda_T^{ij} \;=\;
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}
\int_0^T \mathbf 1\bigl\{|Y_s|<\varepsilon,\;
X_i(s)\wedge X_j(s) \le X_k(s)\ \forall k\ne i,j\bigr\}\,
d\langle Y\rangle_s .
\]

\begin{claim}\label{claim:main}
For the independent Brownian race,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
\E\,\Lambda_T^{ij}
\;=\; 2\sigma^2 \int_0^T w_{ij}(s)\,ds
\;=\; 2\sigma^2 \int_0^T \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial\mu_j}(s)\,ds ,
\end{equation}
where $w_{ij}(s)$ is the static tie density \eqref{eq:tie} of the
time-$s$ race. Equivalently: the Jacobian entry at horizon $s$ is, up
to the factor $2\sigma^2$, the instantaneous rate at which the
leading-pair collision local time accrues.
\end{claim}

\emph{Derivation sketch.} Four steps, one manipulation each.
\begin{align}
\E\,\Lambda_T^{ij}
&= \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\,
   \E\int_0^T \mathbf 1\bigl\{|Y_s|<\varepsilon,\ (i,j)\ \text{lead}\bigr\}\,
   d\langle Y\rangle_s
   \label{eq:step1}\\
&= 2\sigma^2\,\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}
   \int_0^T \Pr\bigl\{|Y_s|<\varepsilon,\ (i,j)\ \text{lead}\bigr\}\,ds
   \label{eq:step2}\\
&= 2\sigma^2 \int_0^T \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\,
   \Pr\bigl\{|Y_s|<\varepsilon,\ (i,j)\ \text{lead}\bigr\}\,ds
   \label{eq:step3}\\
&= 2\sigma^2 \int_0^T\!\!\int \varphi^{(s)}_{ij}(t,t)\,
   \Pr\bigl\{X_k(s)>t\ \forall k\ne i,j \bigm|
   X_i(s)=X_j(s)=t\bigr\}\,dt\,ds .
   \label{eq:step4}
\end{align}
Step \eqref{eq:step1} is the definition of $\Lambda_T^{ij}$ with the
expectation brought inside the limit. Step \eqref{eq:step2} uses
$d\langle Y\rangle_s = 2\sigma^2\,ds$ and Fubini: the expectation of
an indicator is a probability. Step \eqref{eq:step3} exchanges the
limit with the time integral. Step \eqref{eq:step4} evaluates the
band probability to first order in $\varepsilon$: writing
$\varphi^{(s)}_{ij}$ for the joint density of $(X_i(s),X_j(s))$,
\begin{align*}
\Pr\bigl\{|Y_s|<\varepsilon,\ (i,j)\ \text{lead}\bigr\}
&= \int\!\!\int_{|u-v|<\varepsilon} \varphi^{(s)}_{ij}(u,v)\,
\Pr\bigl\{\text{field above } u\vee v \bigm| u,v\bigr\}\,du\,dv\\
&= 2\varepsilon \int \varphi^{(s)}_{ij}(t,t)\,
\Pr\bigl\{\text{field above } t \bigm| t,t\bigr\}\,dt
+ o(\varepsilon),
\end{align*}
the strip of width $2\varepsilon$ around the diagonal collapsing onto
it. The inner integral of \eqref{eq:step4} is precisely \eqref{eq:tie}
for the time-$s$ race, i.e.\ $w_{ij}(s)$. What needs care to make this a proposition: the
$\varepsilon$-limit exchange (dominated convergence with the continuous
conditional density), and the leading-pair indicator, whose
discontinuity set is the triple-tie event --- measure zero for
nondegenerate diffusions, and exactly the degeneracy the Atlas
literature attends to \citep{ichiba2010}.
Correlated (factor) versions keep the time-$T$ marginals inside the
covariance grammar of \citet{cotton2026}; the identity needs only $\langle Y\rangle$ adjusted
for the factor covariance --- mechanical, unwritten. \hfill$\square$

So the boundary flux is not an \emph{analogy} to local time: for
Brownian races it \emph{is} the local-time density, and the shared
field computes it --- implicitly for all $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs, and
explicitly for any one pair --- at $O(nL)$ per horizon.

\section{Measured}

Three checks, all seeded and committed under
\texttt{research/equilibrium/exp2\_localtime} at
\texttt{github.com/microprediction/winning}.

\emph{Claim~\ref{claim:main} against simulation.} A five-runner
Brownian race ($\sigma=1$, $T=0.5$, distinct locations), $60{,}000$
exactly simulated paths at $dt=2.5\times10^{-5}$, occupation estimator
run at band widths $\varepsilon=0.08$ and $0.04$ and extrapolated
linearly to $\varepsilon\to0$ (the finite-band bias is visibly linear:
the raw estimates sit $2.7$--$4.4$ percent low). Against the lattice
side of \eqref{eq:main}, stable under doubling of the horizon grid:
the two favourites, Monte Carlo $0.3539\pm0.0038$ against lattice
$0.3544$ ($z=-0.14$); the favourite against a midfield runner,
$0.0946\pm0.0022$ against $0.0956$ ($z=-0.47$).

\emph{The trace identity.} Summing over named pairs $(i,j)$ gives the
local time of the gap between the ranked first and second particles,
whoever they are, and $J$'s zero row sums make the total a trace:
$\sum_{i<j} w_{ij} = -\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}J$. Measured identical
to six decimals ($0.995801$ both ways). The trace is the sum of the
own-coordinate derivatives $\partial p_i/\partial\mu_i$, which the
forward pass of \citet{cotton2026} returns with the probabilities, so
the whole-field collision rate is free with pricing.

\emph{Scale.} For a field the size of the Russell 3000 index,
$n=3000$, the full expected
rank-$1$/rank-$2$ collision-rate curve across $24$ horizons computes in
$1.43$ seconds on one laptop. Collision local times at this scale are
not otherwise available by any method we know.

\section{Index leakage}

Fernholz calls the relative-return loss a capitalization-league
portfolio suffers when stocks fall out of the league and are replaced
\emph{leakage}, and expresses it through semimartingale local time at
the dividing rank \citep{fernholz2002}. Index entry and exit ---
reconstitution turnover --- is a rank-boundary crossing, so the
machinery above prices it, once the boundary moves from rank one to
rank $k$. The static object generalizes cleanly: for
$q^{(k)}_i = \Pr\{X_i \text{ among the } k \text{ smallest}\}$ the
same divergence-theorem argument puts the whole derivative on ties of
$i$ and $j$ \emph{at the cutoff},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:topk}
\frac{\partial q^{(k)}_i}{\partial \mu_j}
\;=\; \int \varphi_{ij}(t,t)\,
\Pr\bigl\{\#\{m\ne i,j : X_m < t\} = k-1 \,\bigm|\, X_i=X_j=t\bigr\}\,dt ,
\end{equation}
with $k=1$ recovering \eqref{eq:tie}. The count probability is
Poisson--binomial over the survivals the field already evaluates,
$O(nLk)$ by the standard recursion. Summed over pairs and scaled by
$2\sigma^2$, \eqref{eq:topk} is the expected local-time rate at the
rank-$(k,k+1)$ gap: the leakage rate of a top-$k$ index under a fitted
race model, at Russell scale ($k=1000$ or $3000$) at the same cost
orders as everything above. Implementation is tracked as issue~12 of
the \texttt{winning} repository; equation \eqref{eq:topk} is derived
here and not yet implemented or measured.

\section{Why the Atlas literature should care}

Rank-based (Atlas) models \citep{fernholz2002,banner2005} assign drift and volatility by \emph{rank}; the semimartingale
decomposition of the $k$-th ranked process carries the collision local
times $\Lambda^{(k,k+1)}$ between adjacent ranks as correction terms,
and in stochastic portfolio theory the size effect and the attrition of
the largest market weight are expressed through them. Market weights
are a choice-probability vector --- the portfolio remark of
\citet{cotton2026} is the same bridge --- and rank-crossing local times
are what the static machinery evaluates, one horizon at a time.

Trade flows in both directions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Computation to Atlas.} Collision local times are
analytically available only for small $n$ or special symmetric cases.
Claim~\ref{claim:main} plus the lattice evaluates expected
local-time densities for the leading pair at $O(nL)$ per horizon for
$n$ in the millions; the removal counterfactual of \citet{cotton2026}
(delete the leaders, re-race) reaches lower ranks. Checkable against
simulated ranked Brownian systems --- an exact-versus-Monte-Carlo
experiment of the kind that reference's benchmarks already referee.
\item \emph{Structure to us.} The Atlas decomposition names the dynamic
inversion: recover drifts from observed \emph{leadership turnover}
(occupation and crossing rates of ranks), of which the static inversion
is the one-horizon shadow.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope honesty}

The verified identity is for the leading pair; the rank-$k$ boundary
formula \eqref{eq:topk} is derived but unimplemented and unmeasured.
Nothing here addresses stationary gap distributions
\citep{palpitman2008}, where much of the Atlas literature lives; the
contribution in prospect is finite-horizon and computational. Citations
are locator-verified only: the sources have not yet been read in full,
and in particular whether the leading-pair (rather than adjacent-rank)
local time already carries a name in that literature is unchecked.
